# Mardi Gras 2012

Well, that weekend was bat shit insane. I’m also astonished I made it out relatively unscathed. It’s also one of the few times you’ll ever hear me say this about anything…believe the hype.

Without going way overboard on tales of bohemian fascination, the immense character of the city, that everyone will have a “New Orleans moment” and it’s way safer than advertized plus you can drink on the street, well, all those things are true. And beads and parade floats and costumes and brass bands and genuine people everywhere, both locals and tourists.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Hundreds of floats like this all weekend; **alt:** Awesome colours on the floats)

After being somewhat terrified at the Muses parade Thursday realizing it was a whole weekend of that, except many thousands more people, I got in the swing quick. Thursday was also the only day I bothered to venture out with my actual camera, so that [gallery is here](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110021059/http://blimpyb.com/gallery/mardi-gras/) (not that pictures do it any justice).

On Friday after proclaiming the day before I wasn’t really big into parades, there I was back again near the start of the route in the garden district jumping up and down like a school girl waving to people on the floats for throws (my total count for the weekend was around 25 strings of beads, 2 footballs, 1 frisbee and 1 plastic cup, and I wasn’t trying). Wandering up Magazine, or maybe Tchoupitoulas, I stopped to chat with a bunch of kids my age who lived in a house right on the parade route. Within minutes the high fives, dance moves, and handle of Jack Daniels being passed around were all flowing freely. I was aided in making fast friends because one of the residents had lived in Saskatchewan at some point, so got Canadian props. Eventually I sheepishly asked to use his bathroom as those are coveted like nothing else on the parade route, then I found I never really left the house once the full drum kit and guitars came out for jamming and the bong started going around, then I have no idea what happened because I forgot to eat dinner, but I made it back to Bessie alright although I must have fallen down a few times.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Sexy shiny dancers; **alt:** Best shot I took, I dunno why)

Saturday poured rain, I won’t bore you with the details of that or Sunday, but Monday, a.k.a. Lundi Gras is the big party day. The cops even show up on Bourbon Street at midnight to clear everybody out, I only made it over there once and it’s not my crowd, there’s so many better places and cheaper bars with more character. Like for example, Parasol’s, rumoured to be where the Po’Boy was first invented and open since the 1950’s. It was there dealing with the one and a half hour wait for two sandwiches with a friend I’d made earlier in the week that we met Frank and Leroy…

Sitting on a sunny stoop across the street as every table was full, a man in his 50s with wild wavy hair and a sequined Mardi Gras visor came over in his wheelchair and informed us he called this “the office” because he’d always sit there and drink with his acquaintances, which was every single person who walked by. Having been diagnosed with MS a few years earlier definitely didn’t slow Frank down, in fact it probably sped him up thanks to her service dog Leroy, a beautiful and friendly red lab. Within minutes, Frank asked if we wanted to see him go, so he went out in the street, screamed “GO!!” and off Leroy went. They have apparently been clocked at 19 mph as you can learn from this [local news segment about the two of them](https://web.archive.org/web/20140110021059/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cL96UfW14Zg).

After hearing of Frank’s plan to run for congress, how he partied with Cyndi Lauper in the 80’s, that he was going on tour with his one man guitar set when his record came out in a month and how he wanted me to get a ramp into Bessie so I could be his tour bus, well, you get the picture. Then the reporter who did the story stopped by with the weather girl for a visit and a drink at “the office”. Then…

OK, that was 3:30 PM and I stayed out until about 2, so you can guess for yourself plenty more happenned, but I’ll stop rambling. I was originally going to say that everyone should do Mardi Gras at least once in their life (well, if they’re young, or young at heart and like to party) but I can tell you this, I’ll be going back for another.

# Joshua Tree Camping

I wouldn’t call it Joshua Tree camping so much as Joshua Tree surviving. With so many miles of vast and pristine desert wilderness (as pictured in [the gallery](https://web.archive.org/web/20110310051228/http://blimpyb.com/gallery/joshua-tree/)) you’d better be prepared for coyotes wandering through your campsite lured by the lamb shanks you’re cooking in the open fire, not to mention rattlesnakes, scorpions and tarantulas, although I was lucky enough to only come across the first creature, and he was nothing but curious. There is no water or electricity at any of the 10 official Joshua Tree camping grounds so you best pack that in yourself. Cell phone reception or internet you can totally forget, which explains why I was incommunicado the last 5 days.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** The views are unreal; **alt:** Joshua Tree in all its glory)

I wouldn’t have even noticed the coyote while I was tending to the fire if it wasn’t for my Quebecois friend Raphael from Trois Riviere. Having rolled in around 4 PM to Hidden Valley campground, the first one you hit from the main north entrance, I saw the sign read FULL, so did a quick survey, saw nothing and wound up staying the night at Ryan. I wizened up the next day at noon when I drove back into Hidden Valley to scope the first open spot that it’s more just because the rangers only come by to flip the sign every few days. There were plenty available so I grabbed an absolutely stunning spot nestled in with some boulders and a view across the expansive desert behind me.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Stunning Barker Dam; **alt:** Daaaaaaam)

After doing the two nearby day hikes of the visually mind boggling Barker Dam and drastically less interesting Wall Street Mill, I was already afraid someone would steal my spot so to return and find a bunch of water and camping gear hidden in the rocks was somewhat disconcerting. It turned out to be no problem at all though as it was just Raphael’s stuff and had been there all along.

He had a great trick of just pitching his tent in amongst the boulders and staying there for free having hitched into camp from Palm Springs after taking the bus there from Vancouver. Joshua Tree happens to be one of the climbing meccas of North America with its huge array of varying sizes and difficulties of boulders to scramble up, and Raphael had been doing just that for a full month. To hear he only spent $250 during that entire stretch definitely put me to shame, but he ate Ramen noodles and canned tuna while I feasted on lamb, corn, roasted red pepper and whiskey, so I’ll take my monetary licks any day.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** The Wild West; **alt:** W.F. Keys was a bad mother...)

Raphael had it figured out for sure. Beyond his tips on the wisdom of not really having to pay because the rangers never came by and screw it because the place was free when he first came 8 years ago before the California budget crisis, he also well understood this fabled “work-life balance”. Six months a year he cycles through Europe and climbs here or in Red Rocks, the other six months he does seasonal work like picking cherries in the Okanagan and king crab fishing in Alaska. Naturally I am told it’s nothing at all like the TV show, they work at most 6 hours a day while living on the beach in July.

As for the park itself, I am torn whether it is actually better than the redwoods in terms of nature and is perhaps the first place of my entire trip I will tell people “you have to go here”. If you’re anywhere near LA, it’s only 2 hours away and more than worth the trip. Everything feels like a scene out of a Clint Eastwood spaghetti western, or to modernize it, Red Dead Redemption (this is the [best video game of all time](https://web.archive.org/web/20110310051228/http://www.amazon.com/Red-Dead-Redemption-Playstation-3/dp/B001SGZL2W?SubscriptionId=AKIAIID3YVG2VFARBOPA&amp;tag=blimpbackg-20) FYI, if you haven’t played it, get it).

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Freaky deaky trees; **alt:** I call this one "Breakdancing J Tree at Sundown")

Wait, I totally just ruined it even trying to compare the place to anything, because there’s really nothing like it. The Joshua trees themselves just blend into the atmosphere after awhile because the place is so much more than that. They were gifted their name by Mormon settlers who saw the branches extended to the heavens so naturally gave them a good old fashioned biblical name, but even I could feel the obvious spirituality of the place. A fellow next to me on the peak of Ryan Mountain exclaimed he felt like he was at the center of the universe. He also happened to be from Vancouver and a Canucks fan, so his quip about the Calgary Flames almost landed him an express ticket to said spiritual heavens via a more direct route down the side of the mountain with a helpful push from yours truly.

Uhhh…too much to say, check out [the gallery](https://web.archive.org/web/20110310051228/http://blimpyb.com/gallery/joshua-tree/), it’s definitely my best yet.

Addendum of interesting facts:

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Wild rock formations; **alt:** Arch rock (they're good at names))

-100 years ago it rained twice as much here as it does now. When I heard about pioneers raising cattle I scoffed at them as insane, but things change and it once actually made sense, although I can’t think of a more desolate, frightening place to live unless I had a ranch with about a dozen people on it.

-A process occurs in the soil here to reverse erosion caused by cryptobiotic bacteria that actually bond sand back together again into a crust.

-Most of the good stuff is in the high desert at the north end of the park, but if you want free Joshua Tree camping (other than the ol’ not paying trick) there’s free BLM lands you can park an RV or pitch a tent on directly outside the south entrance within sight of sign. Backcountry tent camping is also allowed for free almost anywhere in the park as long as you’re 1 mile from the road and register your vehicle.

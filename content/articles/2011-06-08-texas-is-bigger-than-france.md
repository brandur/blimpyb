# Texas is bigger than France

I have had this fridge magnet since my early days working for a Texas based oil services company in 2006, and only now do I realize how true it is. While I believe it was first crafted during the “War on France” via Freedom Fries because they’re a bunch of pacifist, non-Iraqi murdering jerks, it also well-symbolizes the “everything is bigger in Texas” mantra, which includes Texas itself. And you should see the Republic of Texas map from 1864, now _that_ place was big. However, these days whenever someone tells me I should “check out Austin because it’s cool” for the umpteenth time, a little bit of me dies inside.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Texas used to be REALLY big; **alt:** Republic of Texas map)

The drive from Amarillo to Austin is roughly 550 miles, or in Bessie-speak $200 of gas each way, which adds up to half a month’s budget in Blimpy-speak. I am much closer to Denver (not that I intend to go there either). Hell, just the Texas panhandle is the same size as most states (and with less water than probably any of them too).

When I was near Sedona Arizona, for the first time I saw a weather forecast calling for “Dust”. In Amarillo it calls for “Fire Weather”. Not knowing what the hell this is, I Wikipedia it and find their example is [a weather advisory…from Amarillo Texas](https://web.archive.org/web/20110811140130/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Warning). By 7 PM I see a large smoke plume coming from a dusty dry field 10 miles away quickly spreading due to the 55 mph winds and read on the news of evacuations to the south. The actual definition I believe is humidity below 10%, temperature above 90 F and winds greater than 40 mph. I also realized quite suddenly around this point in time the two tornados that _killed people_ on May 24th in Canton and Piedmont Oklahoma are only 200 miles from here, so when the skies started turning green as well, I actually got kind of nervous.

Maybe this is all happening because Repent Amarillo is right and it has nothing to do with global warming? For the uninitiated, [Repent Amarillo](https://web.archive.org/web/20110811140130/http://www.texasobserver.org/dateline/he-who-casts-the-first-stone) is an organization that deems businesses in the surrounding area immoral, then tries to shut them down with tactics ranging from calling the fire marshal to camping outside with cameras to terrorize the patrons. I only made it to three of the spots on their map accidentally, I will investigate more when I return.

I also found out the legendary “Dude, you have no Koran” was born right here in Amarillo because Repent Amarillo is apparently buddies with Terry Jones. The facebook group Amarillo Citizens Against Repent Amarillo jumped 3500 members in one day after the event, which is not only hilarious, but uplifting that not everyone is as crazy as you think even in the so-called Bible Belt. Although I did get a [nice](https://web.archive.org/web/20110811140130/http://092.me/) reply from the editor of the Amarillo Globe telling me they wouldn’t publish my letter refuting one from a pastor the day before who claimed the Bible was “undeniable scientific fact” because my retort was “too provocative”.

That said, a lot of small towns in rural Oregon and Northern California are far more horrifyingly stereotypical American than Amarillo is. I’m convinced anywhere with at least 250,000 people must have something going on, and the Walmarts here are on the outskirts of town, have a lot less people riding motorized scooters when they can obviously walk and they sell whole wheat bread. Honestly, the place is genuinely complex, they got layers and stuff!

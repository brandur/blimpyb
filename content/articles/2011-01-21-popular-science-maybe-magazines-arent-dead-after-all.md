# Popular Science: Maybe Magazines Aren’t Dead After All

Having not read a real magazine in perhaps years, I was genuinely impressed flipping through this month’s issue of Popular Science as I actually learned several things, so I thought I’d share some of them:

[**Genetically Modified Mosquitoes**](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://www.popsci.com/science/article/2010-11/mutant-mosquitoes-nearly-wipe-out-their-population-and-diseases-they-carry)

I had no idea that these had been introduced to the Cayman Islands and succeeded in reducing the mosquito population by 80% to fight dengue fever. Who knows what the consequences are down the road for whatever used to eat them, but who doesn’t hate mosquitoes, so hooray!

[**The “Internet Kill Switch” Bill**](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://news.antiwar.com/2010/06/18/senate-bill-would-give-obama-power-to-shut-down-internet/)

Dubbed Senate Bill 3480 “Protecting Cyberspace as a National Asset”, there’s actually legislation in place right now that would allow the government to shut down the internet “in case of emergency”. Despite the fact this is practically impossible, the idea that they need such a thing and what the real motives are is frightening. Isn’t that what they do in Iran and North Korea? Worse still, it was proposed by Joe Lieberman, the same man who demanded Amazon and PayPal cut all ties with WikiLeaks arbitrarily without any sort of court injunction. I also found a great site on the NY Times where you can [track it](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://politics.nytimes.com/congress/bills/111/s3480), or any other bill’s progress through the political machine.

**The Second Green Revolution**

Think about this, organic farmers and genetically modified food manufacturers working together. At first it sounds outrageous, but after awhile it’s apparent it’s the only way we can go forward. Harvesting land in any fashion is already a disruption of the natural order of ecosystems. As it stands, we pull oil from a hole in the ground, convert it to fertilizer, transport it great distances using more fossil fuels, then pour it back in the ground, it’s not sustainable. We’ve been doing “genetic engineering” for thousands of years by always picking the quickest ripening fruits with the most seeds that are the most climate and disease resistant, and now we’re all techno geniuses, so why not _program_ that into the food from the get go? (Sorry, I can’t find the original article, but [this one’s kinda neat](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://www.popsci.com/environment/article/2009-07/8-farming-solution-help-stop-world-hunger))

[**The “Smart” Basketball**](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://www.bizjournals.com/columbus/print-edition/2010/11/19/state-betting-ball-bounces-right-for.html)

The incessant talk about using microchips to detect when a soccer ball or hockey puck crossed the goal line never really piqued my interest either, but this one’s kinda neat. Put some sensors and gyroscopes inside a basketball during practice and you can scientifically tell a kid “you need 18.2% more backspin on your jump shot”. Kinda smart, kinda too analytical, but definitely enough to make Tim Hardaway cringe. I’m terrible at shooting, but I used to love shouting “you’re a BRICK LAYER” at other people’s missed shots in gym class. I would have been way more in my element yelling “nice 3.7 revolutions per seconds, jerk!”

[**Camcorders with Built-in HDR**](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://www.popsci.com/gadgets/article/2011-01/casios-super-compact-super-flexible-tryx-reimagines-point-and-shoot-camera)

For those of you following some of my [sillier shots](https://web.archive.org/web/20110305162850/http://blimpyb.com/gallery/hearst-castle/attachment/img_2969/) where I stitch multiple exposures of images together in Photoshop to get higher dynamic range, Casio has introduced a camcorder called the TRYX that does this with every single frame on the fly!

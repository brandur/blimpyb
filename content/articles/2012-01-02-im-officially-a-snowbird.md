# I’m Officially A Snowbird

I’m pretty sure the icing on the cake was me spending New Years Eve at a potluck full of 65 year olds at the group shelter at the Little Ocmulgee campground in the middle of nowhere in Georgia. Either that, or me kicking everyone’s ass at dominos until they got too tired and went to bed at 10 PM. Or maybe the $15 round of golf I played with three of them the next day.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** New Years Resolution: Drink more of this!; **alt:** IMG\_7180)

My one saving grace was that not surprisingly, everyone was Canadian. They all get too sick of the cold and make the 15 hour drive from Ontario to Georgia to spend the winter as campground hosts, it’s surprisingly not as far as you’d think, unless you move at my pace. And as a note to future retired self, the campground (or golf course) host/volunteer gig is a pretty good one. Stay as long as you want for free, golf for free every day all in turn for maybe 2 to 3 hours of light chores each day, such as spending 30 minutes touring young Canadian fellows like myself around in a golf cart when they arrive then maybe grabbing them a pile of wood or replacing a light bulb in the bathroom.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Dude was a slick dresser; **alt:** IMG\_7181)

The whole experience was almost frighteningly Canadian, actually. Where at the Sardis Lake campground in Mississippi on Thanksgiving I tried talking to the locals next to me and they were so terrified of my long hair they didn’t even respond, at Little Ocmulgee I’d chatted about Belleville Ontario, the exchange rate and how cheap beer was down here for 15 minutes before I’d even selected a campsite.

To recap since Nashville, which was only a week ago, I’ve gone through Lynchburg Tennessee and the Jack Daniels Distillery, which wasn’t worth writing about because arriving at 2:30 got me a chance to take the tour at 4, although that was the last time I took the camera out so those are all the attached shots. Then on to Chattanooga, where I still don’t understand the significance of the Choo Choo, but it’s a very upscale chic town with a great microbrewery called the Terminal Brewhouse. Atlanta is too sprawling, horrifyingly large and easy to get in the wrong part of town that I just skipped through it, and then on to Macon, where the whole place is the wrong part of town, although I was able to work a karaoke crowd with another rousing rendition of Desperado, which I’ve determined is a hit down here because it’s sort of, kind of a country song.

_(missing image)_ (**caption:** Stay mellow in 2012, y'all; **alt:** IMG\_7184)

So here I am in Brunswick, Georgia, 10 minutes away from the Atlantic Ocean wanting to save it until tomorrow so I can fully savour the triumph of making it all the way across the continent. I’m not planning on zig-zagging all the way back, and have nothing planned beyond a little Florida time next week, but I’m still pretty sure 2012 will have some big things in store for the Blimpster as I have no intention of stopping any time soon.

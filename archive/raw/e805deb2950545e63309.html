Oregonaturemagikal 72

Blimpy On October - 17 - 2010

Sparing you any of the gory details, I knew it was time to empty Bessie’s tanks at a Sani-Dump, so I searched online for the nearest free one, which turned out to be Harris Beach State Park just north of Brookings.  After doing the dirty deed (they had a donation box of all things next to it), I thought I’d take a wander before heading into town.  Seeing a sign for a Marine Garden, I took a look around and thought “well this just looks like a bunch of hedges” so decided to head down a rocky set of switchbacks to the beach below.

The arch has a calming effect

It was quite the amazing scene to have stumbled on through nothing more than excrement-related fluke.  There was a brilliant set of tidal pools filled with hermit crabs, anemones, star fish and all variety of plant life shielded by a large and narrow natural arch that calmed the incoming sea waves to allow the pools to exist.  To the right of the arch was a massive array of mussels and barnacles clung to the rocks in much more turbulent patches of tide.  On both sides out in the harbour there were sea lions and birds galore.

Anemone is hard to spell

The place was truly alive containing some of the most varied flora and fauna I have ever seen packed into what couldn’t have been more than 100 yards long.  I hopped from rock to rock snapping photos like a madman for at least 20 minutes until I finally realized “ohhh, this is the marine garden!”  The lady in the pink hoodie in one of the shots confirmed my suspicion and told me she and her husband had called in sick to work and done the same with their 4 kids in school for what she figured would be the last blast of beautiful weather for the year.  My kind of gal for sure, she had more spunk and curiosity than her 4 teenagers combined jumping around and poking her head in whatever pool she could find.  Mentioning that it was never above 70 degrees this time of year, I replied “oh that’s just because I’m here.”  She giggled at the silliness of the fedora-adorned Canadian, but the funny part is, I wasn’t joking.

I waited 5 minutes for this to happen again, it didn't

For me the highlight was looking at a patch of mussels curiously high up on some rocks and thinking “but how do they get any water or food up there?”  Sure enough the next wave came crashing in right over top of all of them, yielding what has to be one of my favourite shots, although there have been countless over the last 72 hours.  And that’s just the point, 24, 48, 72 in the titles, all of this happened in a three day stretch.  Oregonaturemagikal was meant to be more a description of the photojournal, but then I got to typing, so either way flip through some of the pictures (Umpqua, Gold Beach, Brookings), you won’t regret it.

Bessie likes

Oh, and just to top it all off then I went and parked overnight right on the banks of the Chetco River Jetty in Brookings with the ocean crashing away just a stone’s throw to my left.  I think the view is the first picture I’ve taken inside Bessie looking out the whole trip, and compared to the last 72 hours, yeah, I guess it’ll have to do.  Sigh, now I have to drive to California and the redwood forests, it’s a tough life.

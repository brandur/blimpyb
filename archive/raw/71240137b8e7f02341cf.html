I Love New York Deli

Blimpy On September - 28 - 2010

When I told one of the people at the house I was crashing at about this nifty little New York deli I found in the U District, his immediate response was “a New York deli in Seattle?  I wouldn’t trust that.”  Fair enough, but having never been to New York and in my first big city in the US, I figured it high time to check out some cheesy Americana.  Of course I stumbled upon the place completely by accident, but their advertising is displayed prominently for blocks, so it’s hard to miss.

Bad economy, good deal

Most of this advertising was for their Recession Breakfast, which I liked because coupled with the name of the deli itself, there’s no possible way they were taking themselves seriously.  As I approached, there were prominent American flags, apples and statues of Liberty, but equally importantly a bottle of mustard at each table.  Because of the neighbourhood they were in, this certainly couldn’t have been any kind of tourist trap, but it caught my eye and in I went.

The busy storefront signage

Inside the place seemed a lot more authentic and having already drawn me in with the presentation, they went straight for the goods: meat, cheese and bread assembled in countless variations.  It was also obviously a legitimate deli with a typical glass display counter for ordering by the pound to take home, which the man in front of me had done in a little wax paper parcel.  It took me awhile to get my bearings with the myriad of selections, but the staff that evening was a couple of guys my age who were more than friendly.  Although not a New York specialty through and through like the reuben, I’ve always been a sucker for brisket so opted for a sandwich of that and saved myself $2.  I guess sauerkraut is expensive, what with the recession and all?

Plenty to choose from

While placing my order, I got my choice of side (also from a good selection as you can see from the attached menu board) and went with the potato salad, which I wasn’t even expecting because I neglected to read.  It all arrived quickly as I was the only one getting dinner around 5 PM, so I took it outside to enjoy.  The presentation immediately struck me as bang on: piled high meat vastly outweighing the bread, just the right amount of potato salad and a huge half pickle.  It was worthy of a picture, blog or no blog.  I can’t speak to the NYC authenticity, but I have been to a Montreal deli or two and figure they can’t be that different, and this certainly fit the mold.

The sandwich came plain, just meat and bread, in my mind as it should be, so I proceeded to slather on the mustard as I’ve been known to do.  And exactly as I was expecting walking up looking at the bottles, it wasn’t just yellow French’s but something a bit spicier.  Sure, it could well have been Keen’s instead of homemade, but whatever it was it fit the bill perfectly.  I did find the meat a tad dry, but again for some reason I expected that too and just used it as an excuse to add more mustard, and it definitely wasn’t short on delicious beefy flavour.

Presentation is half the battle

After sitting down and taking a second glance at the plate, I must say the potato salad was slightly off-putting.  It definitely looked like runny slop, just chunks of potato in a thin liquidy sauce, but after the first bite I was perplexed.  It definitely was just chunks of potato in runny slop, no other ingredients, but there really wasn’t need for it, the sauce was one of the most bang-on potato salad dressings I’ve ever had.  Just the faintest hint of mustard, a nice tang and a great accompaniment to the rest of the meal.  Again, I dunno if this is NYC style, but I’m so used to potato salad being thick and having some other random stuff mixed in, I’m still not sure I’m a convert, but it was very nice.

The final curiosity came with the pickle, and again I plead authenticity ignorance.  It was very bright green and looked great, but on first bite this obviously meant less pickling, it tasted like a cucumber dipped in dill vinegar.  That part I couldn’t finish because I was weirded out, but more so full from the rest of the meal at what turned out to be a quaint, tasty little East Coast treat in the heart of the West Coast.

Six Rivers Brewery

Blimpy On October - 22 - 2010

I’m sort of in a quandary writing this review.  It’s a hoppin’ place to hang out at, but their product is, well, god awful.  And to make matters worse, after seeing me snapping shots with my SLR and typing furiously on my smart phone, the owner came over and had a friendly chat with me for five minutes.  But I’m afraid Blimpy can’t be swayed, so I’ll go with my gut, and I’ve got enough fodder for a bonus Manager’s Special post after.

Unassuming entrance

I actually missed the place on my first pass along McKinleyville’s Central Avenue, it could just as easily be a GNC or a cooking supply store with its blue and yellow simple facade.  Taking a parking spot along the adjacent building, I would soon find the large strip of unmarked gravel behind me was also apparently parking, leaving Bessie nice and boxed in for a 6-point exit later.  However, walking inside, the place was big and very well-decorated with light oak furnishings and empty bottle wall linings in both the restaurant and bar sections.

Earlier in Oregon I commented on their allowing children in bars for the good of the economy and general merriment, but I may have to reverse my tone as I didn’t expect this to extend to a six year old occupying the last bar stool.  Almost every table was empty, but everything along the island bar was taken, probably in no small part due to the Giants being on the verge of a World Series berth.  Still, to me if you’re bringing two kids in tow, grab a booth.  Luckily one fellow saw me surveying the scene with my camera in tow and struck up a conversation on good shots I had taken, before I slunk into a table to read the astoundingly poor journalism of the Humboldt State University paper.  He then proceeded to drink two pints, two shots of tequila, go out for a smoke which he put out in the flower pot 3 inches from the ash tray and drive off.

The place is packed at 4:30

As this indicates, the Six Rivers Brewpub is just the local bar that happens to brew its own stuff, ‘craft’ really isn’t in their vocabulary.  I would learn later the town is referred to as McKentuckyville and Oklahoma-by-the-sea by the rest of Humboldt County.  Undeterred, I decided to order a pint of their IPA, recommended by my drunk driving, photography buff fast friend.  It was very dark for an IPA which intrigued me, but upon my first sip, it turned out to basically be a poor man’s Hammerhead.  It was complex and had a mellowed hoppy bite, but for brewing reasons I can’t explain in text, there was no ‘middle’, just the high hops bite, its heavy aftertaste and a watery flavour in between.  The heavy aftertaste got more and more pronounced with each sip (which is why a real taste test should be a full pint) and by the end of it, the thrill was gone.

Midway through the pint I got a chance to catch the waitress to grab a little food in the form of their “famous” wings.  Every menu says this, but here they had a half-dozen different flavours with a 1 to 10 hotness scale, so I was encouraged enough to go for their 10/10 rated Death Wings.  And surprise surprise, they weren’t hot.  Or good.  They weren’t quite fried to the point of being crispy so were very oily and completely drenched in some sort of Death Slop.  The menu promised chillies, habaneros, jalepenos, serranos and all variety of other flavours, but it was really just a bunch of mashed up crap splashed all over the wings, there was about an inch of goo at the bottom of the bowl.  I will admit, my spice tolerance is retardedly high, but just to make sure I wasn’t crazy I actually ate a full spoonful of Death Slop with as many chunks as possible and didn’t feel heat at all.

Mmmm...death slop

Since I really did want to watch some of the baseball game (my original plan in going there) I thought I’d give them one last shot at redemption by trying their Trula Pilsner.  It boasted of using imported Czech hops and being cold aged for 4 weeks, but after one sip for all I care they probably just did it in a barrel in the parking lot.  Maybe Czech people get a kick sending their reject ingredients they wouldn’t dare use overseas, because you’d get stabbed serving this there.  Easily a tie with Rogue Ales Chipotle for the worst pint I’ve had all trip, it had the taste of fermented keg lines and while the first sip was sort of crisp (but with a nasty skunk musk), halfway through I could barely finish it.

Y’know what, the gloves are off now that I’m in retrospective mode, forget this bar.

Taste: 1.5/5

Value: 2/5 (The $3 pints during happy hour were good ‘value’, but I’d pay twice as much for something that wasn’t so awful.  It’s also happy hour whenever it’s raining outside, probably to keep the town drunks kicking around.)

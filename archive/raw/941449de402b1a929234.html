The Six Arms Brewpub

Blimpy On September - 28 - 2010

Having narrowly missed the 3 o’clock bus out of downtown Seattle, I decide to use my spare half hour to try my first Washington microbrew.  I’d already walked past the Six Arms on my way up to Capitol Hill and somehow missed the keyword “handcrafted” on their sign the first time.  Circling back and realizing they were making all kinds of fine ales in-house, I decided to stop in and try one.

The Six Arms Brewpub on Pine

The outside of the bar gives a good indication of what’s contained within.  Gold lettering and a large facade give it an old pub feel, but the metal chairs, bright colours and right angles add a modern touch.  Inside there’s a main floor with plenty of booths and a more relaxed upstairs, both carrying the old brewpub, but in trendy downtown Seattle vibe, which comes across well.  The one drawback is the main floor approaching the bar is too narrow and laid out incredibly strangely, it’s like there’s a waist-high wooden corridor to get to the bar which you aren’t sure is for patrons or servers.  Regardless, I managed to slink and shimmy my way up and grab a drink menu.

Very cool bar layout

They have a very nice drink menu actually, being a brewpub they’ve taken the time to highlight and describe each of their beverages.  Feeling like an IPA (of which they have several) I come across the Hammerhead, which is described as golden, with a smooth finish.  This sounds just about right for me, after taking a sip of some other brewery’s Hopstoopid creation at a house party the night before, I’m not in the mood for being slapped across the face with flavour.  The bar setup itself looks just awesome, copper finish, chalkboards of specials, even the wine is on tap, I like.

Taking my fresh pint of ale and a newspaper outside to the empty patio, I grab the corner seat for optimal people watching, take my first sip and it is good.  I love how “golden” or “amber” when used to describe a beer can mean a specific flavour as much as a colour.  There is a hint of that classic IPA hops bite, but it’s very subdued, more just an excitation of the taste buds.  This is also likely why the beer has a slightly darker colour than a typical IPA, more roasting means more mellowing of the taste.  And mellow it is, very full, almost bordering on a sweetness.  I dunno, it was just a damn fine pint of beer.

Good vantage point from the patio

I also thoroughly enjoyed my spot on the patio.  As you can see I got a nice view down busy Pike Street of people filtering out of work, and what’s more a testament to Seattle than the bar itself, I had one nice 40 year old lady stop and comment on my camera and how her friend just got one, then a younger tourist couple ask if I was liking the beer.  I told them I most certainly was and that they should go in and give it a whirl, which they did, and if you’re in the area I recommend you do the same.

Taste: 4/5

Value: 2/5 ($5 a pint?  C’mon, this is America!  Of course it was so damn good, it’s easily worth it…)

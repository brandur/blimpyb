Mission Accomplished: I live at a bar

Blimpy On May - 31 - 2011

Pulling into the mall in Amarillo and asking at a couple stores “what’s a good bar with a patio?” having spent the last week dry and fasting, I am pointed in the direction of R&R.  I roll in just as the owner is opening and he gives my RV a long glare, which I view as a bad thing, so I walk over and ask him “is this where I go to get a drink?”  He chuckles and invites me in while he gets the place ready.

I quickly learn that a bartender named Jeremy used to live in his RV that looks just like mine at the bar, parked in the same spot, which is why KC (the owner) was giving me the look.  Jeremy also just went to Santa Rosa Lake to start a few months driving to Seattle, so basically my life in reverse.  The uncanny resemblance and KC’s good natured Texan attitude had landed me a place downtown to park for as long as I want, and 5 free shots before the bar even opened to go along with the $1 draft beers.  The place got hopping for a Tuesday night with a really cool young crowd because KC explained to me to run a successful bar “you make people feel at home if they’re gay, black or purple.”  I would later discover the place is classified as a “gay bar” in Amarillo, from what I can tell because they actually let gay people in.

I started writing this from the Walmart in town I fled to after 2 days, but I’ve moved back since.  Day 1 consisted of running into the guy who worked at the mall and told me to go to R&R then drinking with his friends until the sun came up, day 2 involved enough more free shots from KC that I woke up with a pounding headache and the urge to flee.  Apparently fasting only minorly diminishes your tolerance.  Although by the time Sunday rolled around I came in to find KC with his head in his hands barely able to talk around 4:30 PM, which is just how he left the night before.  And I met two people who said “hey, Canadian guy!” claiming I had spoken with them at length earlier in the week, of which I have no recollection and I’m good at remembering faces.

After not doing it my entire trip, this time I mentally planned ahead for what I thought Texas would be like, beyond just “better than the middle of nowhere”, and my preconceptions were generally right.  Rootin tootin good times is about all I can say to sum up the attitude around here.  Screw it, let’s just have a beer, something like that.  People have tattoos that say “Don’t mess with Texas”, which is funny because the only real badge of pride in New Mexico is they serve green chile everywhere, and it’s only an hour’s drive from here.

For those that don’t know, I’m taking a break over the summer to return home to Calgary, but Amarillo will be the perfect place to return to because I’m already a regular with the local grunge crowd having met half the town at R&R and told them Jeremy’s life story even though I’ve never met him.  Hell, I even had a beer with his mom.  Also, when the daytime high is 80, it gets up to about 95 in Bessie, and it’s going to get up to 105 this week.  I’m slightly concerned things will melt when all the windows are closed while I’m gone, but as long as it doesn’t hurt the speakers I’m cool with it.

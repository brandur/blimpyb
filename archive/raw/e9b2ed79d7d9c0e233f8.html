The USS Midway

Blimpy On January - 7 - 2011

Housed in the San Diego harbour next to downtown, the USS Midway has to be one of the biggest naval museums out there just by nature of the fact it was the largest US Navy aircraft carrier for the first few decades after its construction.  Stood upright it would be about as tall as a 60 story building, so obviously there’s plenty to see.  I wasn’t expecting much going in, but found myself pleasantly surprised that I could have easily spent more time there after being booted out at sundown.

The real naval base is across the harbour

While you can walk around the engine rooms, sailors quarters and the like, the real treat is the flight deck up top with a vast array of US fighter jets from the Korean War to the present.  Named after the famous battle of Midway against the Japanese in WWII, they actually had to borrow the name from an already commissioned ship.  This is traditionally bad luck, and sure enough the ship the name was borrow from wound up getting sunk.

Other interesting facts include planes launched from the Midway downing both the first and last enemy planes during the Vietnam war, even though it was decommissioned for a lot of that conflict for upgrades.  Also, something that has always boggled my mind as standard aircraft carrier construction as you can see from the attached pic, the runway is only 200 feet long.  A blast shield pops up for the jet propulsion to push against while cables also catapult the jets off the end of the boat, sounds like a wild ride.

Regardless, I really just wanted to highlight the gallery, and while I wish I had my wide angle lens at the time, there’s plenty of cool shots, especially as the sun starts to set.

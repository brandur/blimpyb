Oregonaturemagikal 24

Blimpy On October - 15 - 2010

Everyone who’d been told me the Oregon coast was gorgeous, and don’t get me wrong, I believed them.

OK, this cave is pretty cool

Working along Washington’s coast into Northern Oregon, I was definitely impressed, but by the time I’d reached Tillamook I was starting to think “yeah, I get it, expansive big beaches and grasslands.”  But the locals told me “just wait until you get a couple hundred miles south of here” and they were right.

Isn't this dune country?

Hopping back from Eugene to Florence was definitely the best drive of the whole trip, so we were off to a good start.    The agricultural mini-prairie of the Willamette valley quickly yielded to huge pine trees, rolling hills, valleys and lakes.  Bridges were eventually replaced with a long tunnel through sheer rock that popped me out right at the basin of a large inlet and harbour shrouded in fog with some of the logging town charm I’d experienced earlier.  It wasn’t until my ascent to the Sea Lion Caves the next day I realized how truly different the coast was down here with its huge jutting boulders, abound with a whole new variety of sea life.

AHA! Thought so.

Deciding to use my casino winnings for a stay at a State Park with running water and showers (it’d been awhile), I chose Umpqua Lighthouse because it was more focused on tenting sites and would be cheaper and more remote.  This certainly proved true as I got an amazing site and basically a quarter of the campground all to myself (luckily the opposite quarter from the drunken Oklahoman trailer trash screaming songs from Grease at the top of their lungs around 11 PM until the ranger told them to stuff it).  Within minutes of pulling in, I investigated my spot, looked down the hill behind it and saw a shimmering through the trees not 100 yards away.  I thought this was supposed to be dune country, and it was, just on the other side of the excellently trailed 1 mile circumference fresh water lake surrounded by old growth pine forest.  Oh, and 200 yards up the hill from the lake was Oregon’s oldest lighthouse.

It's also lake country

Refreshed, I set off for a driving day, going as far as logic and laziness would take me, as usual.  Stopping mid-afternoon for gas at an old fishing town called Bandon that was trying to pretty itself up by now calling itself Bandon-by-the-sea, I had to laugh at the lipstick on a pig aesthetic.  There’s two incredibly touristy blocks they’ve tried to isolate from the rest of the whole town, but the RV parking is always off hidden somewhere, in this case right next to the Arcade Tavern.  I wandered the tiny place briefly, realizing it was Sunday and wanting to watch football for a bit I found the bar on the tourist side sold $4.50 pints and charged $11 for a plain hamburger.  Returning to the Arcade, I found they sold $2 pints and a pulled pork sandwich with cheesy potatoes for $5, you even got to see the woman in the back with a crock pot and aluminum baking tray.

Oh right, there's a lighthouse too

Of course this was after the semi-record scratch moment of me walking in wearing sandals and a bright blue t-shirt.  At least half the guys at the bar had a shot and a beer, slouched over them like they were their only two friends in the world, that is when they weren’t screaming “HIT ‘EM!!” at the TV or threateningly challenging each other to pool.  If Ziggy had walked in and dropped his pants, I wouldn’t have batted an eye (that’s a reference to The Wire, FYI, watch it).

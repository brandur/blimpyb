Bill’s Tavern Brewhouse

Blimpy On October - 7 - 2010

One interesting aspect of my perception I’ve noticed with this whole trip is trouble dissociating experiences.  If I have a tough drive or it’s pouring rain, I generally don’t enjoy whatever I do for the next while immediately following.  As I was saying about Cannon Beach being the most pretentious place on my entire voyage, I would say that tainted my experience of Bill’s Tavern, but looking back, no, the place is decidedly below average.

Classic exterior

A mere block away from the Visitor Information Center I’d returned to the following day to steal WiFi, Bill’s Tavern is a small brewpub with a simple interior and a few patio seats, but contains the magic words brew, meaning they make something in-house I cannot experience elsewhere.  Sidling up to the bar and laughing in my head about the phallic nature of their taps, I take a look at the plainly written chalkboard of beers and grab a food menu.  Then stand there for two minutes, when there are two bartenders looking right at me and chatting to themselves in a bar with two tables occupied because it’s Monday afternoon.  I guess they have better things to do than take my money.

(Modern?) classic interior

When one guy finally wanders over and spurts “yeah, can I help you?”, I ask him about a couple of their beers and decide on the Blackberry Wheat, it being the most unique.  I tag on a fried oyster sandwich and let him know I’d like to sit on the patio.  “The patio?  Oh, can you leave a credit card, y’know, just in case…you want to open a tab?”  He didn’t ask this when he assumed I’d sit inside.  I might be wearing a hoodie and look a bit greasy, and I guess it speaks to the town as much as me being a fish out of water there, but frankly I was offended, I’m not that dishevelled.  “No, just settle it now, that’s definitely all I’ll be ordering thanks.”  I felt like stealing some silverware or a pint glass just to play the part.

Carrying my own beer and glass of water out to one of the open tables, with a decent view of one of the main intersections in town, I sat down and had a sip.  Blech.  We used to make this British drink called Snake Bite when I was 19, it was half cider, half beer and a shot of black currant extract.  That’s what their Blackberry Wheat tasted like.  I have no insight to their brewing process, but having already sampled several raspberry beers made with real raspberries, this one just tasted like flavour syrup added to crappy beer.  To make matters worse, it was borderline flat.

Really tasty

I will say the fried oyster sandwich was their saving grace, but that would likely be delicious damn near anywhere.  Other than being a tad heavy on the tartar sauce (but hey, this is America) the breading was bang on, the oysters were succulent and cooked just right.  I enjoyed that about 1000 times more than the beer, which really isn’t a good sign for a “brewhouse”.

It’s pretty simple really, a loose-money, over-the-top tourist town doesn’t really give a crap because they’ve already got your money and figure you won’t be coming back, which I won’t.

Taste: 1.5/5

Value: 1.5/5 (The prices were fine, but service is part of value)

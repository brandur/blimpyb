Beware the Rainbows: An Ode to the Turkey Vulture

Blimpy On February - 2 - 2012

The more life, the more death, the more vultures.  Effortless in the gentlest of wind, feathered wings spread wide, always exactly an hour before sundown they begin to soar by the dozens, one must always be the first to take flight, the rest following within seconds.  Their penchant for carrion unseen belies the lushness of the Florida forest.  Discordant with most wildernesses across the continent, the escaped housecat in feral form is the dominant species, but it shrinks from view in the light.  The alligator does so accidentally as a result of its nature; its thrashing prey’s death throes at sundown far too loud to have ever feigned camouflage.

Vultures are fucking hippies.  Everyone else does all the work, they spend all their time high as the goddam sky swooping in for a freebie whenever they can, and I love them for it.

Acton, Florida

But the tick…upon finding the first ever in my life latched to my thigh, I plucked it while musing “I have fabricated the evolution of man from antiquity to modernity solely within my own mind, the internal combustion engine facilitating my journey of thousands of miles, only to be the vehicle for the greatest distance you will ever travel, from shrubbery bush to squashed fingertip residue.  At least I have the capacity to pretend I have hopes and a will of my own, and to perhaps falsely ascribe the same virtues to the vulture.  But you tick, are an automaton, feeding without knowing.”

***

Having done “civilization” for far too long, I decided to return to my western US roots by boondocking in the middle of nowhere in Florida’s Ocala National Forest.  Problem is, being an hour from Orlando and the ocean in Daytona Beach, this isn’t the middle of nowhere like, oh, all of New Mexico.

Lake Eaton

My sanctioned $8 a night campground hosts at Lake Eaton told me of a couple free places to park in the middle of the forest, but warned me to watch out for “rainbows”.  I had already come across what that meant in Florida-speak researching the area online, so proddingly asked “you mean gypsies?”  To which I got the amazing response “no, worse…hippies.”  Fucking hippies!

Funny thing is, they use that term pretty loosely, it really just means vagabonds with unwashed hair, and there’s a huge difference.  When I heard tales of bonfires, yelling and drinking until 1 AM, that sounded like something to seek out, not avoid.  Yet when I drove 3 miles down the highway and saw at least a half dozen people with dreadlocks, a pitbull and their thumbs stuck out on the side of the road, I realized this unsanctioned campsite may very well have been the mistake the 60 year olds made it out to be.

Scowls.  Scowls that I knew of their perch in the bush where the ranger only stopped by once a week to boot them out.  A scowl like I got giving an extremely (and accidentally) quizzical look to the man who walked into Burger King a week ago in Daytona Beach demanding free food with the unspoken threat that he would either start screaming uncontrollably or shit in the middle of the floor.  Oh he got his food, the manager even knew his name, but I know now that he was a rainbow, not a hippie…a tick, not a vulture.

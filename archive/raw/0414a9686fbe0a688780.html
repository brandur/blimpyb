Solvang Brewing Company

Blimpy On December - 2 - 2010

I am not at all used to reviewing breweries when it’s freezing outside, so the cozy warm environment inside this brand new brewpub was downright endearing.

Windmill city...errr, town

Solvang is a small incredibly touristy Danish town in the Santa Ynez valley, essentially the non-coastal route between San Luis Obispo and Santa Barbara.  And it is most decidedly wine country, so it’s nice to see a beer option popping up.  In one of the leftover windmills kicking around town (there are several, to complement the clodhopper and Hans Christian Andersen statue in the center of town) they threw in some vats and opened for business about a month ago, and from the looks of it on a Sunday night they’re already doing well due to the lack of places to eat or drink in town after the sun goes down.

I took a bunch of camera phone shots that looked even worse

Not that it’s that kind of place, it’s very bright and open and well decorated with leather booths and bar stools and everyone told me the food was great, but I’d already eaten.  Well, maybe it is sorta that kind of place, all the patrons seemed to know each other, even the one who fell right out of his barstool in dramatic fashion after having a pint of each they had on tap.  The bartender just turned up the pop country as though no one would notice, so it wasn’t really a big deal.

As for their beer selection, although they only had three of their own brews ready so far (amber, wheat and porter) the auxiliary selection was perhaps the best I’ve seen my entire trip.  As per my belt buckle, I’m already a sucker for Dogfish Head, and as per my Canadian roots a sucker for Trois Pistole, and, well, how many breweries in southern California have beer from rural Quebec on tap?  Not to mention I got to tell a fellow ordering one the story that comes on the side of the box about how God and the Devil came to Trois Pistole to battle it out and somehow came out with the beer.

A tasty list

Also, both of these brews are great belly warmers clocking in around 9%, and the 70-something lifer beside me told me it was as cold as he’d ever remembered in the valley.  Ordering a pint of their amber, I found it had many of the same characteristics.  I’d actually go so far as to say it was complex and powerful enough to remind me of a Belgian Dubbel.  It was strong with a medium roasted malt, quite spicy and even had a little alcohol sear to it, which was quite welcome.

After chatting at length with two girls my age working out a business plan to buy Valley Books across the street, and a decidedly Hollywood-type bleach blonde, collagen lipped 35 year old who drew me the worst map in the history of bar napkin maps to everywhere I had no intention of going, I swilled back the rest of my Dogfish Head 90 Minute IPA and was pleasantly surprised to find out one top notch house crafted ale and one Delaware imported microbrew only ran me a grand total of $9.  Thoroughly satisfied I returned to my RV for some Z’s, totally expecting to get rousted by the cops on such upscale, Christmas light lined streets, but instead had the best sleep imaginable for a 25 degree Fahrenheit night with no heat.

Taste: 3.75/5

Value: 4/5 (Very rare tap selection, really friendly atmosphere)

Cape Disappointment was a real letdown

Blimpy On October - 2 - 2010

Having already missed the towns of Humptulips and Tokeland, I had to take a jab here.  Hey, in my hometown one of the major roads is called Shaganappi, which phonetically translates to having sex with a diaper in British slang.  There’s a ghost town in Oregon called Idiotville that I’ll try to stop at.

Delicious personal safety

Pulling into Washington’s Long Beach peninsula, I immediately got the uplifting impression that I wasn’t going to get murdered and eaten like Westport.  Everything was much brighter, there were people everywhere, it was a weekend, all was good.  The woman at the tourist bureau ran down some sightseeing and RV parks for me, then when I asked her if there was a free spot, she leaned over the counter and whispered to me “don’t tell anyone I told you, but just go out back by 7th and Oregon Street”.  A brilliant revelation indeed, find an RV park, then just park right behind it.  The owner was sitting on her trailer’s front porch, I think I could almost see her mouthing “asshole” as I pulled in, but I’ll gladly be a $30 richer asshole any day of the week.

Original West Coast gangstas

After discovering the mind-blowing 50 McNuggets for $9.99 (don’t worry, I didn’t actually buy them) and wandering around the towns of Seaview and Long Beach for awhile, I decided to head down to Cape Disappointment State Park and the Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center.  Just as an FYI, those two dudes are HUGE here at the mouth of the Columbia.  I must admit if you’d asked me a few years ago I probably would have told you one of them was Jerry Lee Lewis and the other one might be Dick Clark.  But seriously, the dudes were badass, traversing the entire continent two centuries ago I respect.  Naturally, Americans respect them more, ad absurdum.

Stupid football fans

Cape Disappointment State Park is actually complete bullshit though.  There’s real cool views and nice forest walks, but its setup in general is the worst of any “park” I’ve ever seen.  First off, I have 4 different maps and guides I picked up that show countless historical sites to pull off at, and at no point in any of them is there mention that every single one says “No RV’s Past This Point”.  Slightly peeved, I continued down the windy and slanted roads to the Interpretive Center, hoping to learn a thing or two.  As you can see from the sign, and lack of pictures, I chose to learn nothing instead.

“I tell you Clark, one day people will marvel at our grand adventures, erect countless statues and name universities and highways after us.  But what makes it all worthwhile is knowing they’ll pay $8.50 to visit an interpretive center about us, when they could learn it all for free on something that will be called the interwebs.  And chicken nuggets will be 5 times cheaper than in the British Empire!”

“Shut up Meriwether.  And just because you keep referring to us in last name only doesn’t mean they’ll forget how prissy your first name is.”

Nothing says nature preserve like a mansion

Other “highlights” of the “park” include the fact that there are incredibly lavish and expensive private residences right in the middle of it, along with a US coastguard station whose intercom is loud enough to be heard in the middle of serene nature with tidbits like “John, please come to reception for a package delivery”.

Seriously, skip the whole place and cross the bridge to Astoria and check out the infinitely superior Fort Stevens State Park in Oregon.  I even took some pics for a gallery so you don’t have to waste your time.

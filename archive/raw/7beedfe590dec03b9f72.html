Blimpy Video #2 Explained

Blimpy On May - 24 - 2011

I think I said I was going to do this for the first video then never did, so considering this one’s about 500 miles more worth of footage, I figure I should at least say a few points.  If you haven’t watched the vid yet, check it out here in 1080p.

-When the crazy breakdown starts going after the church shot about 5 minutes in, that is an abstract critique of religion.  I don’t believe the apocalypse is coming any time soon (although my timing was good with the nuts this weekend) I just kind of wanted to point out the freaky shit that happens when you let a bunch of dogmatic zealots run wild for 2000 years…creepy as hell bronze statues of the 12 stations of the cross and atom bombs.  Hell, I didn’t write The Book of Revelations, that’s why James Earl Jones reading it is sort of supposed to be out of context and creepy.

Told ya I went easy on ya

-My head was spinning after the Albuquerque Atomic museum, so I figured yours should be too, sorry if you don’t like grindcore music.  I actually went easy on you, that segment could have been twice as long and I intentionally excluded all the shots of melted bicycles that said Nagasaki, so you can thank me later.  Did you know it took 10% of the power output of the entire US from 1942 to 1945 to refine the uranium and plutonium for three bombs?

-It has been the windiest spring in 20 years in the New Mexico desert and it’s already one of the windiest places in the country, which is a) why the sound is terrible in some spots and b) why I used it to signify impending made up video doom.  The wind is blowing at 55 mph in Amarillo as I write this.

-Speaking of sound, next time I will turn my voice up.  I wanted the music to be louder as it has more punch than my ramblings, but all that did was make me hard to hear and add clipping to Mozart’s Requiem (Lacrimosa is that section in case you were wondering).  It’s hard to remember when editing that I obviously know exactly what I said so subconsciously feel it’s loud enough no matter what.

The first of several shots you never got to see. Petrified wood is 5 times harder than granite.

-I omitted several hundred really cool pictures and several potential stories from the blog starting all the way back in Arizona because I didn’t want to ruin the surprise of the intense landscapes and scenery I was experiencing.  In order they include the Petrified Forest, El Morro, El Malpais, Albuquerque, Santa Fe (and the Gearfest electronic music festival there), Cadillac Ranch and the Amarillo Botanical Gardens.  Still, uhhh, it’s the goddam desert, that’s about everything there is.  Oh, and you can’t buy alcohol for the 120 miles before Albuquerque because it’s all reservation land.

-Tucumcari itself is the largest broken down hellhole I have come across.  It’s one thing to have been cooler in 1880, and to have at one time actually been a Route 66 stop, but to still have 6000 people living there is downright depressing for them.  I didn’t take any pictures in the place because I would have got jacked for my camera.

-Here in Amarillo everything is The Good, The Bad and The Ugly themed, and a restaurant sold the Lee Van Cleff pulled pork sandwich.  I told the waitress they had misspelled it, and she disagreed, to the point I had to say “trust me, it’s Van Cleef, I would know,” while pointing at one of the movie posters on the wall to back me up.

-After Effects is a lot of fun, but takes a lot of time and computer horsepower.  That cartoon shot of me unleashing the birds took 3 hours for my computer to render into an actual watchable video file, the sand at the end about an hour and a half.  I had a full other minute worth of footage I was going to make “freaky deaky digital”, but it served no purpose other than showing what’s out there…similar to the stuff I left in.

-I totally forgot to tie back in the Hagakure reference in the cartoon segment, so I’ll end with my favourite quote from the whole book which also relates nicely to the video itself.  “When one thinks he has gone too far, he will not have erred.”

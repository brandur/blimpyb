Redwood Curtain Brewery

Blimpy On October - 24 - 2010

This place is a straight up brewery, and I loved it.  It’s got a tasting room that’s only open certain times, certain days of the week and doesn’t get overcrowded or too noisy.  The co-owner and bartender that particular afternoon Eli is a bright, friendly and generally awesome guy.  And you can see the vats where the beer comes from right beside you, they even have to hang a rope so you don’t walk where you aren’t supposed to.

It's only hard to find cuz they're sneaky like that

Having only got the space in April, Redwood Curtain is essentially a brand new, up-and-coming brewery in Arcata and servicing the surrounding Humboldt County.  The owner spoke a few times about them approaching the brewing game with slow growth, word of mouth and internet buzz as their business model.  I will try and oblige on the third (here’s their website), and it seems to be working well for them so far.

One thing to note about the Redwood Curtain Brewery is you won’t be able to find it.  It’s in an industrial park on the outskirts of Arcata by the Interpretive Center for the marsh, but there aren’t any signs except above the front door.  I asked about this and was told it’s on purpose, Redwood Curtain, they don’t want to be a tourist attraction or get too big for their britches and they just finished pouring concrete for their equipment so brewing is the priority.

Most accurate descriptions I've seen

And brew they have.  Focusing on Belgian style beers for the most part, it’s their niche within the already niche microbrewery scene, as Eli explained to me.  I would sit and chat for a good half hour with him on a Saturday afternoon and learned a ton about brewing, Humboldt County and California.

Starting with a little taster of the Belgian Style Triple, it really was well-rounded as the menu states, and gave me a sort of warm and fuzzy feeling, not wholly due to the 9.6% I assure you.  The menu lists IBU (International Bitterness Units) but now another new one I have yet to see, OG.  This stands for Original Gravity and indicates how much sugar is in the batch before fermentation.

For whatever reason, I came in really wanting an IPA, so naturally got a taster of Imperial Golden Ale as well first, and again it was quite tasty and just like the menu says, malt forward, they describe it better than I can.  Ending up with a taster of the IPA before going for the full pint, I was impressed by it to, especially after learning their speciality was supposed to be on the Belgian beers.  It was a darker golden in colour and in style reminded me of the Hammerhead I quite enjoyed, but was just…better.  The hops were more pronounced, the flavour in general was fuller, and I really wasn’t milking it when it took me I don’t know how long to finish as I savoured each biting sip.

Up close and personal

One other key manoeuvre I learned here is that I should probably flash the Dogfish Head belt buckle I’ve worn since visiting their brewery in Delaware in 2005 more often.  Its owner Sam has published a book about Brewing Up A Business and him and Eli had crossed paths before, giving us one more thing to talk about beyond Prop 19, elected versus appointed public officials, the BP spill and capitalism in general.  You see, that’s what a good beer is really about.

Unfortunately Eli also knew how to read the prevailing weather conditions and let me know the picking up wind meant all hell was soon to be unleashed, so after reluctantly packing up without having another pint, I barely got to my destination before the skies opened up for a day and a half.  My only other regret is not getting one of their to-go mason jars for while I weathered the storm!

Taste: 4.25/5

Value: Infinity?/5 (You can’t measure people power is dollars)

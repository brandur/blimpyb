Bessie Breakdown #2

Blimpy On July - 22 - 2010

This time I couldn’t even put my own drive shaft back on if I tried…

2 miles to a town without a store

Setting out from Christina Lake to make it to Osoyoos by mid-afternoon, I knew I was in for a nasty stretch of mountain pass.  A continual uphill for about 30 clicks, then a rapid descent into the Okanagan valley that I’m sort of glad I never got the chance to drive down myself.  About a half hour past Grand Forks, I noticed that while Bessie was going uphill, she would lose speed being such a tubby hunk of metal pulled by a 1984 350 engine.  This has happened before and she’d downshift into second gear (it’s just a three speed transmission) except this time around it seemed to cause her pain and she would stutter a bit before catching.

Again, I chalk it up on the “old RV” pile and keep driving.  Except soon the same problem is happening on the flats.  Not comfortable with the continual lurching, not being able to make it into third gear and topping out at 65 km/h, I pulled over and let her sit for 15 minutes, assuming the transmission was hot.  I started her back up, made it about another minute, noticed the same problem and pulled over halfway up a steep hill to call the AMA.  Except apparently one of the shortcomings of the small mountain hamlet of Bridesville is that they have no cell phone reception.

I thought of walking that way instead, but thought better

Looking back down the hill trying to figure out where I just passed, I see some red and yellow in the distance and think “yeah, that must have been a Shell station”.  So I go trudging down, and within seconds of entering the row of houses realize it’s a school bus and a red barn, there are no stores here.  Luckily the first house has a man watering his garden with a beautiful pale eyed wolf-husky cross, he lets me use his phone and even drives me back up the hill.

When the tow truck driver arrives 30 minutes later (Jon, nice fellow) he says he’s having trouble getting the drive shaft universal joint bolts off.  He grabs some more tools, I hear some banging, he emerges and off we go.  Getting dropped off behind the Okanagan RV Center as it’s closed on Sunday, he says he can’t put it back on either because the bolts were stripped.  Fair enough, I’ve played this game before, I can do it myself.  After laying out the tarp, getting the socket set and the parts he took off, I immediately notice that the banging I heard was Jon smashing everything that wouldn’t come undone to bits with a hammer.  This I cannot fix.

The tree behind the Okanagan RV Center in Osoyoos is crazy

The repair shop isn’t able to book me in until Tuesday, but they are able to put Humpty U-joint back together again, take her for a spin up the hill and let me know there’s no damage to the transmission, but I should change the fluid because it’s old and put in a new filter.  Out of the house next door to the shop comes an 80-year-old man looking to chat it up with me while he does some yard work.  He informs me he got the same done to the transmission on his RV and it ran a lot smoother and got better fuel economy because there was no slippage.  Hmmm…preventative maintenance saving you money?  Quite the concept.

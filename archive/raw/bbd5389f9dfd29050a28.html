The Redwoods, The Madness

Blimpy On October - 21 - 2010

Why oh why did the very first trailhead I hit along the Nathan B. Drury Scenic Byway have to have a sign about the redwoods outlasting the dinosaurs.  Sure, they tempered it with a John Steinbeck quote about his “silence and awe”, but I’d describe it more as fear and trembling.

See, they're big

There are trees more than 1500 years old, over 250 feet tall and weighing a million pounds, this you probably know.  It’s a sight to behold, and one I had great difficulty capturing on film because they’re too tall.  Being a Wednesday afternoon, there was nary a soul within miles as I was free to stroll between the giants, both alive and fallen, far more alone with my thoughts due to the stimulus of greatness and history than I am within the confines of my RV.  And do you want to know what echoed through my mind as I wandered in complete silence amongst the ground level ferns and mosses, shaded by the out-of-reach pine needle canopy above?

“You hear that, Mr. Anderson?  That is the sound of inevitability.”

Even the "Ever Living" title is scary

That’s right, the redwoods (and that damned dinosaur sign) reminded my subconscious of Agent Smith and his prescience.  It’s so preposterous it’s incisive.  This has been a recurring theme my entire trip, I just chose not to write about it to save freaking you out.  I’ll try and keep it light, but the general gist is that the rise and fall of man is inevitable and we are far past the crest.  I would discuss this at length in Vancouver with a few friends, have it mentioned in passing in Seattle, then find complete strangers in my hosts in Portland were true 2012 believers.

The redwoods and their timelessness make this all perfectly clear.  The dinosaurs didn’t even do anything and they were obliterated, compare that to what we’ve been up to since the Industrial Revolution.  Ice ages occur roughly every 50,000 years with or without our intervention.  The most populous part of California will fall into the sea without any help from Lex Luthor.  Crescent City will be ravaged by another tsunami.  And the redwoods will still be standing.  They are linear and know no better, we are exponential and do know better, yet continue.

The uncarved trunk is more worthy of attention

I saw someone had carved their initials in the trunk of a quite fair sized tree, certainly over five hundred years old.  I had the camera all framed up to take a shot so I could post a picture with a caption along the lines of “how would you like it if I carved ‘Sequoia 2010’ on your forehead”, then realized their callous actions deserved no such immortality on film.  I’ll leave it to our ape masters to read and laugh a good Dr. Zaius chuckle.  Or perhaps Mel Gibson’s school children army, although I’m not sure they could read.

None of this is meant to be morbid or fatalist, it just is.  I’m a realist, I don’t know what you are, but I’m afraid even in the semi-utopic future Lando Calrissian sold out to keep his mines going.  And this is coming from a guy who can harness the powers of the force when he wears his fedora.

Really puts things in perspective

I’ve obviously intentionally scattered pop culture references wherever possible because it takes the edge off as well as providing additional insight in less words (other than Agent Smith, that really happened), but the conclusion here is simple.  There is a most aptly named “Big Tree” in Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park which is the largest for several miles.  In the late 1800’s the man who owned the land wanted to chop it down to turn it into a really big, cool looking stump of a dance floor.  The big-hatted, gun-toting Texan on the Simpsons would be proud, there was even an episode about just that.  Before we understood what environmentalism or conservation or sustainability meant, that was our preternatural instinct.  Sure, we can try and “teach” and “ingrain” these “concepts” into people from birth, but the next time you see an attractive member of the opposite sex walk by at the bar, tell me which one wins.

Album of the Week: Free Your Mind…And Your Ass Will Follow by Funkadelic

Blimpy On July - 21 - 2010

The title track of this album is essentially the title track to all of the PFunk philosophy.  Sorry, philosophy is semi-absurd here, more like enlightened stoned ramblings.  But the lines from its opening that you will hear me frequently repeat are ones to take to the grave.  “Free your mind…and your ass will follow.”  “The kingdom of Heaven is within.”  “Open up your funky mind, and you can fly.”  “Freedom is free of the need to be free.”  Think of it as meta-freedom, let your mind wander through it and float above it, open up.  This song is the best loose definition of Blimpy’s essence I have found to date and why it had to be the first Album of the Week.

Free Your Mind...And Your Ass Will Follow

What really makes this album is the strength of the music easily matching that of the message.  Obviously just with the band name itself, let alone the history, everyone will immediately assume this is funk music, which is not strictly the case.  I always liked Funkadelic more than Parliament for that reason, even though they largely shared the same band members.  The early 70’s were a heyday for the melding of all things psychedelic, which in the case of Funkadelic yields a more acid rock or blues feel (not to mention the name of the band).  The guitars are syrupy but still shred, the keys are up front and pounding while the drums and lyrics fade in and out of a haze shrouding the music’s still very funky skeleton.

A lot of it has to do with the prominent use of the electric guitar.  I’ve already alluded to the Jimi Hendrix influence on Michael Hampton, both in terms of style and sound as well as the large ‘fro, silk scarf and copious amounts of LSD.  It gives the music far too much edge to just be funk or soul, and instead becomes a little bit of everything freaky from its day.  Call it swamp funk if you insist on keeping the moniker.

One track worth mentioning in particular for treading new ground is Funky Dollar Bill.  Not only did this precursor a lot of PFunk’s government and establishment satire (“Our Fathers, who art on Wall Street” came years later), it had a much more lasting influence.  Sure Bob Dylan already wrote Maggie’s Farm, disenfranchisement in society has high and rising and I doubt Funkadelic was commenting on the introduction of central bank-backed currency during the reign of King Henry VIII, but godammit if they didn’t make the whole concept cool as hell.  Part of the reason I wrote this was to make sure people knew PFunk was a lot more than a bunch of Dr. Dre samples.  Part of the reason they’re so heavily referenced is because of this marriage of music and message.  Jimmy Spicer’s Dollar Bill Y’all, Wu-Tang’s C.R.E.A.M., even Limp Bizkit’s Three Dollar Bill all draw inspiration from this same pool decades later.

So there you have it.  Six tracks of searing freakiness adding up to one hell of a timeless album, and for some people, a way of life.

You can pick up this album at Amazon for $16.68.

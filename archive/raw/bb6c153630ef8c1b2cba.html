Blimpy’s Famous Ribs

Blimpy On August - 12 - 2010

It all started a few years back when I was watching cooking shows on TV and noticed them all using spice rubs on meat before putting them on the grill.  OK, I can do that.  The next logical step was dabbling in making my own BBQ sauces.  Fiddling with the combination of those two ever since, this not too difficult recipe is one I can make with my eyes closed and always comes out juicy, tender and delicious.  Plus, you can cook it in a fire pit to keep with the theme (chuck roast, bourbon ginger ale bacon).

Serves 4-6

Flavour central

Ingredients

  • 2 racks pork side ribs (back ribs are less fatty but more expensive)
  • 6 tbsp paprika
  • 1 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1.5 tbsp mustard powder
  • ½ tbsp crushed black pepper
  • 1 tbsp chilli powder
  • ½ tbsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp chilli pepper flakes
  • 1 tsp dried parsley
  • 2 tsp sea salt flakes
  • 15 whole white peppercorns
  • 1.5 cups generic brand plain BBQ sauce
  • 1 jalapeño pepper, diced
  • ½ inch thick slice of red onion, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, diced
  • 1 tbsp bourbon molasses mustard

Preparation

To flog a dead horse in my meat selection rationale, one of the best things about pork side ribs is they are dirt cheap.  Back ribs can run up to twice the price, and while they are less fatty, slightly more tender and generally more meaty, the key to this recipe is cooking slow which allows the fat to drip off and gets the meat really tender.  When selecting a cut of side ribs, it’s incredibly important to closely inspect how much fat versus meat there is as it can vary wildly.  Also the number of ribs and their length can vary, so just make sure you get the leanest, meatiest package of a size that suits you.  Leftover uncooked racks can always be frozen for later.

Coated in goodness

Taking the ribs out of the package and laying them face down you’ll see the underside has a thin skin or membrane on it.  If left on, the spice rub won’t stick on that side and it will turn into a thin, crispy shell causing the ribs to curl up while cooking.  Ideally you’ll want to remove it but this can sometimes be tough to do.  Grabbing a loose edge and pulling with even pressure perpendicular to the bone all the way along its length should work fairly well, and working a sharp knife in between the skin and the meat will help loosen it also, just be careful not to take the meat off with it.  Once you’ve got the skin removed you’ll want to trim as much of the loose white chunks of fat off as possible.  Then, depending on the size of the racks versus the grill (always check first!!) you may need to cut them in half.

Now comes the important part, the rub.  This is where most of the flavour in the dish comes from, I’ve eaten these ribs with no sauce and not complained one bit.  Unfortunately I have never actually measured the ingredients, I just sorta free pour because you generally need quite a fair bit of powder, but the ingredient list should be pretty close, or you can judge from the picture, or make your own tweaks.  Sometimes a little brown sugar is nice, maybe some thyme if you want that earthy flavour.  I was a bit heavy on the paprika this time around, but that’s not much of an issue flavour-wise.  Simply combine all the dry ingredients in a flat tray large enough to roll the ribs around in and cover well.  If you run out with half a rack to go, it’s always real easy to pour in more.  Once thoroughly coated, you’ll want to let the ribs sit for 2 hours before cooking, any longer and it will start to permeate the meat too much and become overbearing.

Cooking

Multi-tasking the fire pit

These ribs can be done three different ways: BBQ, oven or fire pit.  On the BBQ you’ll want to use indirect heat so turn one side of the grill to medium/medium-low and place the ribs on the other side with the lid closed.  In the oven you’ll want to set it at around 275 degrees F.  In the fire pit you’re just winging it, but same idea, not above flames, temperature again somewhere around 275.  For all three of these, the cook time is 2.5 hours, slap ‘em on removed skin side down, you don’t even have to rotate them.  Badda bing.

Oh right, the sauce!  So once you’ve plopped the meat down on the grill and have all this spare time, enjoy a couple beers in the sun!  Sorry, I meant BBQ sauce.  Rather than go through a whole convoluted recipe from scratch (canned tomatoes, molasses, it can get involved) instead I’m just going to throw you a real life tip on how to make kick ass BBQ sauce of your own choosing real quick.  The key ingredient is store-bought generic BBQ sauce, get the cheapest bottle of Kraft whatever with no particular flavour, then spice it up with your own.  My traditional method is to take the diced onion, garlic and jalapeño and mellow them for about 20 minutes in a saucepan with oil on low heat, then just stir that into a bowl of BBQ sauce along with a spoonful of my fancy mustard.  Your imagination is the limit once you get the simple concept, I made one by adding strawberries and lime as well which was even better, but a bit palette extreme for most.

My sauce was weak, but the awesome flavour was strong

So now that the ribs are slow-cooking away and you have a bowl of sauce, when do they meet?  Again, up to you.  If you like a “cooked” sauce where it’s slightly browned and bubbling and really oozing into the meat, you can slather it on the ribs with about 45 minutes remaining.  Or if you like the sauce to maintain its saucy consistency, something like 25 minutes before you take them off will work better.  Once you hit the magic 2.5 hour mark, you want to make sure you take the ribs off onto a platter and tent (cover) them with tin foil so they can rest for at least 10 minutes.  This is essential for this recipe as it reseals all the juices back into the meat, which you will immediately notice as you take your first juicy bite.  Napkins are an essential.

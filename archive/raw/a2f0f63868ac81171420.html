Hearst Castle at San Simeon

Blimpy On November - 30 - 2010

I can’t think of anything to say about the place beyond spectacular.  The more I write, the less justice I would do it, so I’ll let the gallery do most of the talking.  They only give you about a minute in each location, so my framing’s a little rushed, but the subject matter makes up for it.

Probably actually from ancient Rome

America’s first true media baron, William Randolph Hearst earned the equivalent of around half a million of today’s dollars every single day.  The estate that Hearst Castle is on was kept in the possession of his mother until her death in her 80’s, never allowing William to build on it for fear it might get “out of hand”.  How right she was.  When you’re standing inside the place and you look around and think “those are pretty nice ceilings”, it becomes all too real when you are told by the tour guide they are 700 year old ceilings imported from a church in Spain.

On the website and all the promotional info, they keep making a huge deal about this 45 minute making of the castle video included before the tour, and I thought it would be some cheesy 1940’s highlight reel thing, but no, it is stunning.  I’ve never seen so many helicopter flyover shots of European castles in crystalline high definition on what may as well be an IMAX screen.  The main point of the video is that William Randolph Hearst really wanted to build a Gothic and Renaissance style full blown castle, and he did, trust me, you’ll never see anything like it, especially in any of those lame sauce real European castles.

Yeah, I guess I'd swim here too

Another plus, after visiting the place I’m absolutely dying to watch Citizen Kane again, which is of course based on the life of William Randolph Hearst.  Orson Wells is already the cream of the crop in terms of filmmakers in my books so him I do not doubt, but I need to find out why the dejection that was so clearly a central theme of the movie was so completely whitewashed from my mind after seeing the lush and beautiful confines of Hearst Castle and the thousands of acres of land it sits atop of.  Why do you need a sled named Rosebud when you’ve got wild zebras prancing about?!  They’re still there today!  Wild zebras!

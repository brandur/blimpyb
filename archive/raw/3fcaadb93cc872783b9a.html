First run-in with the cops

Blimpy On October - 6 - 2010

Actually the first and second run-ins occurred within hours of each other, but both were delightful experiences.  See I have this policy of not (blatantly) breaking any laws in the US, so the 5-0 are actually my friends, I just want to spend money in their fair country.  But not at RV parks.

Walking back to the public lot I’d stopped at in Seaside, Oregon to watch the sunset on the beach, I found a local police officer standing there surveying Bessie.  Hey, the sign said no parking after 11, I had several hours buffer, so I struck up a chat.  He said much the same, that he just wanted to do an early check on who he could come give $700 fines to later.  Yikes.  I asked him if he knew a free spot to park and he basically said “uhhh, somewhere else down the road” but pointed me the direction of a highway turnout near Cannon Beach.

Wait a second, I know that guy

Of course being the dumbass I am, I broke one of my rules of not driving at night.  Look, I’ll trek 7 miles down the road to save $20.  Except when it’s pitch black it’s hard to see the turnouts, so I wound up in town instead.  Cannon Beach is definitely the most hoity-toity, touristy place I’ve been in yet, as a fellow from the golf course bar warned me the night before.  Everything looks like a cookie cutter tourist chalet with timber facades and the names of trees referenced like they’re going out of style.  Intending all along to turn around, I made it about 50 yards down main street, when the bright blue and red lights came on behind me.

Turning into the Visitor Information Center parking lot (ironically), the cop got the hood spotlight and his hand flashlight on me and let me know “THIS CONVERSATION IS BEING RECORDED.  I AM A ROBOT.”  Okay, not the second sentence.  “License, registration, proof of insurance.”  I hand them over, he asks if my license matches my current address.  Naturally I say “uhhh, this is my current address” and he immediately lightens up and says something like “right on, may as well use it if you got it!”

I’d been pulled over because one of my brake lights doesn’t work…but only when I turn the headlights on, so he let me off with a warning.  I used the opportunity to say I was glad he’d stopped me so he could tell me where to park.  Again, nowhere in the city limits, $700 fine, but he gave me real good directions to the same roadside turnout and said that’s where they kicked people to usually.

Then as he walked back to his car, he quickly turned back and inquired “hold on just a minute, my partner saw you drinking something, you know we have open container laws in Oregon, right?”  I hoisted my 3L water jug, make a joke right to him about how it’s actually straight vodka and we both part ways laughing out loud.

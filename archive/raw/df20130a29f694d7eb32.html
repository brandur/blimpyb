Rogue Ales Public House

Blimpy On October - 11 - 2010

Randomly stumbling upon this place in downtown Portland’s snooty Pearl district should work well.  I can review a microbrew you can actually buy yourself, Rogue is international having seen their bottles throughout my journey and before it even began.  They’re having a weekend-long Columbus Day sale and a keg can be had for $100 or a 12-case of 22 ounce bottles for $22, far better than the $5 for one I had seen previously in Canadian liquor stores.  Not that I really think the product is worth it at either price.  Unless you like paying for hype and image, perhaps.

Fairly normal exterior

The place is genuinely packed at 5 PM on a Saturday, barely an open table in the large, multi-roomed joint.  One is a dining area and restaurant style, one is bar stools and high tables, one is converted to a store room for the sale, but some tables in the back are left open for board games.  I ask about the taster tray and find it’s $4 for 4 of my choice, but am told they’re soooo busy I’ll need to wait 10 minutes for one of the decorative wooden official trays.  The bartender hands me a slip of paper to write down my choices, they have at least 20 different house brews.  It’s a good system, I fill it out and am immediately handed back a decorative wooden official tray with my choices?

Highly abnormal interior

Taking it outside for some breathing and elbow room, I grab the one table that’s under an awning as the rain’s threatening to start up again.  First up is the Juniper Pale, which is short on hops so the juniper stands out too much, even though there’s not enough of it either.  The second is the American Amber, the best of the lot as it’s got a strong red taste but is still clean and crisp.  The third is their signature Dead Guy Ale, at the Columbus Day sale these are still retailing for their normal $32 for 12 due to supply and demand.  Thing is, the stuff just tastes like beer.  I mean yeah, it’s a bit malty, but when push comes to shove, it’s just beer.

Apparently I'm lucky to have gotten one

My final choice was a calculated misstep, the Chipotle Ale.  I’ll do the same at a sushi restaurant, order the weirdest thing they got and have my friends say “but that just sounds off?”  Of course it does, and it usually tastes bad too, but I have to try the unexpected at the off-chance it’s some sort of flavour epiphany.  The Chipotle Ale is not.  Whatever they’ve managed to extract from the “it” food ingredient of the last few years (and the reason I’m convinced it was used as an ingredient, not actual merit) comes through as a rank muskiness, stuck deep in the flavour of each sip.  You know the one tap nobody drinks and when you get the next pint of it there’s that brass staleness of unused lines?  That’s what Rogue Chipotle Ale tastes like.

I just want to go to the bathroom

To top it all off, all the beers were slightly warm and slightly flat.  I don’t think it could be because they’re that busy running through kegs, they would have developed a system by now.  Maybe they did and the system’s just not working.  Or maybe the system is that the patrons that aren’t tourists are generally oozing excessive amounts of cool and just want to be seen there between the surfboards and motion activated cackling skulls lining the walls in between racks and racks of merchandizing.  Y’know what I’m sayin?

In case you don’t, I’m also referring to the food menu for dogs, cheesy slogans of “Dare. Risk. Dream.” and “So You Want A Revolution?” and bathrooms labelled Hops and Barley, requiring you to ask someone cool enough to know which is which.  Now do y’know what I’m sayin?

Taste: 2/5

Value: 2.5/5

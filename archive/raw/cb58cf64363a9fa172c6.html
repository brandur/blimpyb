Blimpy’s Hollywood Insider

Blimpy On December - 24 - 2010

I spent my time in LA living with the assistants to directors and producers in Hollywood.  Getting home from work at 9 PM was pretty much standard so the stiff drink to immediately follow was well earned.  Running someone else’s life is a task I would not be suited for considering I barely bother to run my own.

The hype machine pervades the city

Beautiful, self-assured and well educated women, but not paid enough of course.  In conversation they would all gladly tell me their salary, complain that it is low but go on briefly and directly on point that it’s supply and demand because countless others would die to have the same job.  They all obviously could have cashed in going a completely different route, but realized they’d rather do something they loved, and always loved television and movies, so here we are.  Success is measured up matter of factly with “any day I get to meet Steven Spielberg is a good day.”

Naturally this is counterbalanced with what to me sounds like a very real threat of being fired at any moment.  Fired for interrupting the wrong meeting for reasons ranging from delivering a pack of smokes to letting the boss know Larry David is on the phone.  Fired for making the wrong call on a celebrity who RSVPed to the Christmas party invitation that allowed for +1 with “I’ll be coming with 8 of my girls”.  Getting the right price for the ice sculptor and correct number of champagne bottles is the least of their worries.  Writing a letter to the president of BMW because the boss’s brand new $250,000 ride had a footprint on one of the floor mats is downright trivial.

I’ve gained a massive appreciation of how showbiz is all guts and no glory.  Every glitzed up, dazzling star on the red carpet or big budget blockbuster they are hocking is held up by the blood, sweat and tears of literally thousands of everyday people.  LA is one of the least judgemental cities I’ve visited.  Everyone’s balancing their personal battles with their grand aspirations, trying to make a buck, getting that everyone else is too.  It’s an amazing, if ultra-realistic American scene.

But most of the city is this

I quite liked LA because it’s exactly what you’d expect.  You need a car to get around the massive sprawl of freeways and are told going 15 mph on them at 4 PM is actually pretty good.  Even mentioning taking the bus anywhere is scoffed at as dangerous lunacy.  I’ve never seen so many El Caminos.  The old school hip hop radio station has ads to become a “rim design technician” or to get better insurance rates at Big Time by telling them “Mac 10 sent you”.

Hollywood itself and Sunset Boulevard are run down and busted up, littered with cell phone stores, smoke shops and burrito stands.  But right next door the 3 or 4 touristy blocks have tours, museums and costumed figures charging to take pictures with them by day and some of the ritziest night clubs imaginable by night.

Back where I was staying one of the girls told me if things didn’t brighten up within six months, she’d give up.  I told her I already had and she immediately rebutted that no I hadn’t, because I at least had a clue what came next.

I just turbo-charged my GPS

Blimpy On October - 2 - 2010

For those of you linking straight to this page and not seeing the main page slider, the title is meant to be a joke, the first image on the right shows why.  Beer.  User-generated GPS points-of-interest courtesy the internet.  Yes.

Hell yes

Two hours of work last night, and I’ve amplified and re-invigorated my adventures anew, to the point I had trouble sleeping due to excitement, and I really didn’t need the shot in the arm.  Up to now, I sort of have rough ideas of where I’m headed, maybe somewhere to be in a month and I figure I’ll just stop at what looks cool along the way.  But without stopping at every single tourist information center or googling each town I traverse, how am I to know what’s cool?  This becomes combinatorially more difficult when I try to extrapolate three towns in advance out of a choice of twenty.

The real impetus for my research was hoping to find some decent boondocking information so I wouldn’t wind up repeating my Westport nightmare, instead having logical and already tested places to put Bessie.  Quickly I was lead to boondocking.org where I was delighted to find a GPS coordinate file of places that people have successfully camped at before.  As I’m already using Microsoft Streets & Trips 2010, I knew there must be a way to import or overlay this data, and ohhhh ho ho there is (it takes GPX and CSV files, but this post isn’t meant to be technical).

Next I was able to find a points-of-interest (POI) megafile with a plethora of information.  Walmarts, rest stops, state parks, shopping centers, all kinds of useful stuff, and not just free places to park.  Factory tours?  Museums?  Even waterfalls?  Wait a second, these sound like fun things to do that weren’t even on my radar.

Information overload, in the best way possible

Further googling led me further down the rabbit hole to an absolute goldmine at poi-factory.com.  Thousands and thousands of user-submitted GPS map listings of whatever suits your fancy.  This is where I found the beer map (to the tune of 6000 breweries) and the very next day it led me to the Fort George brewery in Astoria, Oregon which I otherwise would not have known existed.  Golf courses, ghost towns, abandoned amusement parks, your own tastes and imagination are the limit.  I only started with a dozen different types to not overload myself (illustrated in the attached picture) but will certainly add more.  My current personal quirky favourites are all the locations visited on the show Diners, Drive-Ins & Dives (I hate the host, but love the concept) and the UNESCO World Heritage Sites (apparently my home province of Alberta has the same number as the rest of Canada combined, suck on that).

Originally setting out to find free places to camp I can now do that with ease, but even better I can plot a really awesome course days in advance with nothing but a quick glance and a few double clicks.  A lot of the GPS files come with excellent descriptions, even click-through hyperlinks to more information about places if you have web connectivity, or phone numbers that can be dialled with the click of a button via Bluetooth if you’re lucky enough to have a phone.

Seriously, if you’re going travelling damn near anywhere and you have any sort of GPS device, this is an absolute must.

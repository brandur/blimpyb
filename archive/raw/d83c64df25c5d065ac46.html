The Smartest American I’ve Met

Blimpy On October - 8 - 2010

“You like the bumper stickers?” he asked as he walked out of the used car dealership and saw me taking a picture of them.

“Very much” I replied, quite honestly.

An awesome array of statements

“Which one’s your favourite?” he continued, and I told him the one in the middle about fascism coming draped in a cross and a flag.  “Mine too, and people around here give me a lot of grief for it.  Funny thing is, it’s a quote from a religious man.  But they still don’t get it, Americans are dumb as shit, this country is straight fucked.”

He went on at great length and we agreed on every single point, it was as surprising as it was fascinating.  Rural Oregon is the first place I’ve been where that America resides, the one that the man who would introduce himself to me as James was railing against and that I will ultimately be slithering into the belly of in Missouri.

James did most of the talking, and for a lot of it I just held my tongue and nodded, almost afraid someone walking by would accuse me of heresy and treason, even though the heavy-set 40-something man I was discussing it with would be the true guilty party.  In rapid succession he told me he was an atheist but thought people were free to believe whatever foolishness they wanted, that he was a liberal democrat but thought libertarianism had several merits that should be added to the public political discourse, that he still attended an anti-Iraq war protest here in Tillamook every Friday because people had forgotten it was still even happening and that he had no choice but to support Obama because he wasn’t senile, knew what the internet was and didn’t have a she-devil as a running mate.  This was all before I could tell him I was Canadian.

This is not freedom

“Oh, you’re one of those socialist scumbags” he sarcastically shot at me.  I chuckled and replied “but we’re not even socialists, take a look at Scan…” “…danavia, I know” he finished the sentence for me.  “But nobody else has a goddam clue, believe me.”

Wanting to get in some bit of my own to show I was fully on-board, I explained to him my current beef on “Freedom” not being about flags and fried potatoes and he countered with the fact that only 20% of Americans even have a passport and few ever left their home state.  “Exactly!” I jumped in excitedly, “I quit my job and live in a van to do the exact opposite of that!”

He's a flip-flopper!

My favourite moment was him recalling downtroddenly explaining to like-minded tourists how Bush could have won a second term, when I quickly interjected “at least he’s not a goddam flip-flopper!”  James scrunched up his face and knowingly nodded while looking down at the ground, almost embarrassed.

He even went on to talk about green energy and that a store in town completely powered by its own micro wind and solar generation was ridiculed by some of his friends because “solar doesn’t even work”.  HA!  I had a good laugh, James hadn’t even seen I was with Bessie yet, I pointed to her roof and said “oh yeah?”  We both shook our heads and chuckled under our breath as James’ nephew came out of the dealership and I was forced to bid them both adieu to attend a family dinner.  James may not actually be the smartest American I’ve met, but he definitely was the best at getting a dazzling array of intelligent points across in under 5 minutes.

Reflections after the fact?  The world needs more James, I really wish I could see what the peeled off sticker used to say, and I totally should have got that dude’s number.

Album of the Week: Blitz ‘n’ Ass by Jahcoozi

Blimpy On January - 13 - 2011

Having rekindled my electronic roots last week with Supermodified, I realized I should maybe dwell on my bread and butter a tad longer.  This time around we’ll be looking at German glitch hop/dancehall trio Jahcoozi’s second album Blitz ‘n’ Ass.

Funnily enough, this album has two characteristics I usually find quite off-putting.  The first is the almost blatant style biting from MIA.  As soon as the vocals drop on the opener BLN you’ll notice the similarity; Jahcoozi’s MC Sasha Perera has the exact same born in Sri Lanka, raised in Britain pedigree as Maya Arulpragasam.  Not only that, but her sly yet erratic delivery is belted over bombastic, heavily synthesized beats, again all too familiar.  However, despite the perceived sameness with MIA, I’m more than willing to let Jahcoozi slide because to my ears it’s ultimately a different sound after repeated listens.  The music isn’t nearly as cluttered and comes across with exceptional clarity and punch.  As for the lyrics, there’s a much greater emphasis on fun and sexuality, and while it is political, there’s none of the misguided, pseudo BS kind that ultimately caused me to completely disregard MIA as an artist, despite how much I enjoyed Piracy Funds Terrorism and Arular back in the day.  Jahcoozi isn’t interested in generating controversy for its own sake to build hype, they just want to make hard hitting electronic music.

My second gripe with this album is that it takes awhile to catch its stride with the second half being much stronger, and even then a few of the tracks are forgettable.  The opener BLN has jump and is catchy, but sounds like something I could have heard years ago, making it good but not great.  However, by the time the third track Gameboy gets rolling, its underwater thump, handclap syncopation and use of vocals as a melodic and percussive instrument to tie everything together has me hooked.  There’s even a simple message about kids these days sitting on the couch playing video games rather than going outside and playing with real people, but it’s nothing more than cute while letting the music speak for itself, which I greatly appreciate over MIA’s thinly veiled call to impeach all the world’s politicians while supporting the Tamil Tigers.  Not that there’s anything wrong with either of those goals, until they’re used to sell records.

By the time the record reaches my favourite cut Takin’ Your Street, I’m also reminded why I don’t recommend electronic albums all that often: you need to have a good stereo system to hear half the notes.  I can’t even imagine listening to this song on headphones as I sit here with my entire RV pulsating, jumbling my internal organs while generating strange looks from passersby on the street.  Maybe they’d understand if they hopped in to hear the full spectrum of the song’s fading in and out interplay of clicks, clangs, growls and scratches.  Or on second thought, maybe not, I woke up this morning to find a note on my windshield that someone had called the City of San Diego on me…

The album finishes with several good tracks using a bit more relaxed sound, but still maintaining the exceptionally crisp production found throughout.  All in all, while Blitz ‘n’ Ass isn’t genre defining, the standout tracks are absolutely searing electronic very useful for showing off how pimp your RV stereo system is to the point you are asked to move it, and also generally a lot of fun.  Also recommended for anyone who likes the MIA sound.

Nelson Brewing Company: Paddy Whack Pale Ale

Blimpy On July - 12 - 2010

There’s a reason you’ve never heard of this beer.

I made the beer look better than it tastes

In my first small town on my grand adventure, looking forward to making a tradition of sampling the local brew, I snagged a six pack of Nelson Brewing Company’s Paddy Whack Pale Ale.  It had been 30+ Celsius for several days, so I was looking forward to kicking back with some tunes, reading a book and enjoying the IPA I intentionally chose assuming it would be light, but still have a ton of bite and not go down like water.

My immediate reaction to it touching my lips during the first sip was “oh my this is beery”.  Not in a hearty flavourful way, just strong.  Reading the 6.5% on the label, I guess I’ll let it slide, may as well swallow.  Sloshing the mouthful back and having it touch the back and sides of my tongue yielded a different, equally knee-jerk reaction of “oh my this is hoppy”.  I actually sat there perplexed at what just happened.  This one-two flavour punch wasn’t a surgical Mike Tyson left jab, right hook where you were left completely finished on the ground, but with no choice but to admire the dazzling fury.  No, it was more like a WWF tag team match where two fat guys in spandex just chest bumped with you in between, and it didn’t really hurt, it was more just embarrassing to be a part of.

The worst part is, after about three sips, my mind and body numbed to whatever was going on in this strange brew and it just tasted like a strong dose of nothing in particular.  Keyword strong.  The best way to explain it seems like they were making test batches, came out with one that was very beery, so said “hmm, let’s throw in a bunch of hops and make an IPA!”  I know the brewing process doesn’t work this way, but the proof is always in the pudding.

I don't think this is even the brewery, but it was closed and I ain't goin back to find out

Lastly, I don’t understand organic beer.  OK, we’ve got a bunch of barley and hops that have never been within 100 miles of fertilizer and had geishas massage them every day.  Then we take them and throw them in an old dirty barrel and let them rot into a neurotoxin we drink.  What’s the point…unless you’re in Nelson and the word organic is purely related to marketing and not health or flavour?  I’m just sayin.

If you want a smaller craft IPA from the area, definitely go with Hop Head from Kelowna’s Tree Brewery or the Industrial Pale Ale from Calgary’s Wild Rose Brewery.  You’d probably even be better served with a Sam Adams.  Who knows what ingredients they put into it at the factory in New Jersey, but they taste better and cost less and that’s what matters.

Taste: 2/5

Value: 1/5 (sorry but with HST and Liquor Tax, I don’t see any BC breweries scoring too well at $14 per six pack)

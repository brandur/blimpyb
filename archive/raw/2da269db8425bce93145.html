An Afternoon in Aberdeen

Blimpy On September - 29 - 2010

It didn’t take long to find the America a lot of people warned me about, and was certainly expecting myself.  Of course being aloof as always, I neglected to think an old logging town and old fishing town would be the ideal spots.

The view back up the way into town

Having departed Seattle with the goal of heading up the Olympic Peninsula to rainforest country, I made it to Aberdeen by late afternoon.  Aberdeen is the hometown of Kurt Cobain; the Welcome To Aberdeen sign’s tagline is Come As You Are, that must make it cool.  Wait, didn’t he leave there for Seattle, then eventually shoot himself in the face?

As I pulled into the mall at the edge of town, I had no intention of eating at McDonald’s despite the complete lack of them in Seattle, but they had 2 quarter pounders for $4, that’s half as much as Canada!  And a two lane drive thru, that’s twice as many as Canada!  Inside they had ketchup on tap and free WiFi.  Still smartphoneless, this is useful to know as my planned stop off the highway in Tacoma at a Verizon store was in a district with only Asian signage and a salesman who’s English seemed along the lines of “3G!  $200!  You buy now!”  No, I buy at the next Walmart I find.  But it better be a Supercenter, those regular Walmarts like the one in Aberdeen don’t do hi-tech.

Nothing going on

With a bit of time on my hands, I decided to wander for a bit then stock up on supplies.  Even I was surprised to see a cardboard sign toting beggar at each of the 4 roads in and out of the parking lot.  Whatever, let’s try this “River Walk” thing.  I think the signage should be changed to “drunken hobos and grungy teenagers who stare intensely and chirp at people with a camera and a funny hat”.  It’s broad daylight, there’s people around, no biggie, let’s try Main Street instead.  Oh shit, after a 5 second look, this is obviously one of them dead towns.  I know it sounds insane, but I came across the distinct odour of alcohol at least 5 times in my hour wandering the town, this is rarely a good sign.

George Scott, kind of a big deal

Of course now that I’ve also got the much sweeter scent of the harbour, I remember telling everyone before I left I couldn’t wait to just park next to the ocean and sit.  Coupled with the rainforest being an hour the wrong direction, and the possibility of hills (Bessie’s arch nemesis), I change my mind and am going straight to the Pacific.  The map points me to Westport, the cashier at Walmart says it’s just lovely there, and it’s only 15 minutes away as the sun’s about an hour from setting, so off I go…

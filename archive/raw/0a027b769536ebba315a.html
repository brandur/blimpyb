California Election Special

Blimpy On November - 2 - 2010

Today is voting day across the US and with San Francisco being a very politically charged city, I thought I’d outline some of the major races and ballot initiatives because there’s a lot.  As you may know, Arnold Schwarzenegger was allowed to run for governor because they got enough signatures to get it on the ballot to boot their governor at the time before his term was up, so the propositions can carry a lot of weight.  I also picked up a handy Voter Guide from the San Francisco League of Pissed Off Voters that I have attached.  It’s kind of frustrating it just tells you what to do, but hey, everybody’s got an agenda.

Makes a good centerpiece

Governor – Jerry Brown (D) VS Meg Whitman (R)

Meg Whitman is the former CEO of eBay and an uber rich, power hungry capitalist, which likely speaks to who she would appease to if voted in, let alone being a Republican.  She has already run the most expensive self-funded gubernatorial campaign in history using an estimated $163 million of her own $1.3 billion personal fortune.  She was on the board of Goldman Sachs and has admitted to making millions spinning IPOs she had access to before the public.  She admitted she didn’t vote at all for a 28 year period.  She thinks illegal immigrants shouldn’t be allowed to attend California schools, yet her housekeeper was one.  She wants to suspend California’s environmental legislation and doesn’t support gay marriage.

Luckily Jerry Brown is ahead in the polls.  He was California governor in the late 70’s and early 80’s, and his father was governor for most of the 60’s.  I have no idea why American’s love a dynasty so much, but at least he’s a Democrat and not Meg Whitman.  He came second in the 1992 Democratic presidential primary to Bill Clinton, has been the mayor of Oakland and is the current California Attorney General where he launched a lawsuit in 2008 against Countrywide Financial for unfair lending practices in the mortgage meltdown.  He has supported green energy his entire career.

Prop 19 – Legalization of Marijuana

Much ballyhooed, I originally was going to try and capture some of the reaction to this race on my blog, then Arnie beat me to the punch by decriminalizing it.  The idea is that pot becomes legal and regulated so the state can make tax revenue on it, which is especially needed by the massively in debt California which has the lowest credit rating of any state.  It’s also obviously a stoner’s dream and supported widely by the youth population.  Seniors and 40 something Generation Xers with teenage kids oppose it.

The summary version

Naturally though, there are always strange hidden machinations behind the scenes.  A lot of local growers I encountered in Humboldt county oppose it because of the commercialization threat to mom and pop operations.  People like Richard Lee, creator of the Oaksterdam University for medicinal growing in Oakland are heavy backers of the bill because they have the ability to instantly mobilize large scale commercial manufacturing and profit immensely.  Phillip Morris wouldn’t be far behind.

And there’s also the gigantic mess it would create immediately following passage.  Could anyone walk into a medicinal dispensary?  Would they sell doobies at 7-11?  The DEA says they would still enforce federal statutes within California.  There was once buzz of federal funding being withheld from the State, and while that has subsided, there is obvious tension.  But for my two cents, that seems like a good thing as one of the major benefits is media attention and possibly spreading the idea to other states.  People I spoke with in Washington and Oregon both said they decided to hold off until 2012 to push for the initiative because more young people vote in presidential election years.  I still hope Prop 19 passes, but something tells me it won’t.

Prop 23 – Suspend California’s Anti-Global Warming Legislation

This one is freaking brilliant.  California voted in 2006 to somewhat follow Kyoto and greatly curb their CO2 emissions, amongst other measures.  One campaign ad will show people laying solar panels or building windmills and fuel cells and say it’s a job creator, another will talk about cap-and-trade and taxation and say it’s a job destroyer.  So it somehow wound up on the ballot to suspend California’s 2006 environmental bill until the State’s unemployment rate is cut in half!  Great wording, they may as well say forever!  Problem is, regulation of emissions and green energy incentives cost money, which California doesn’t have.  But the Texas oil companies backing the bill have plenty!

The long hand

Prop 25 – Allow the State Budget to be Passed with a Simple Majority

In California’s State Legislature, a two thirds majority is required for the budget to pass, or to raise taxes.  Since the Democrats don’t like cutting services and the Republicans don’t like raising taxes, it has lead to a stalemate each of the last several years, sometimes reaching the point where the government had to pay its employees and distribute tax returns in the form of IOU’s.  All this bill is trying to do is get the budget itself back to a 50% + 1 situation so they can actually pass it and not have to spend as much time every year bickering over it.  At the same time, it would potentially allow increases in certain fees and charges (not quite taxes) but I mean hey California, you’re massively in debt, you either have to raise taxes, cut services or go further into debt, it’s your call, but naturally people want none of those three.

Prop L – Illegalize Loitering and Sitting on the Sidewalk in San Francisco

They have both state and local props, and this local one’s good for a laugh: make it illegal to sit on the sidewalk.  San Fran’s a nice town, it stays warm enough, there’s enough hippie vibe going on, so naturally there’s a fair few homeless people.  They don’t have places to sleep, so they go for the sidewalk.  At the same time, San Francisco is an incredibly rich city, so someone in the upper crust decided they didn’t like seeing this while they went to get their latte, and figured they’d use the sledgehammer approach to what is a wide sweeping societal issue.  Newsflash, you’re trying to make being homeless illegal and it won’t work!  I can’t imagine this passing, and the backlash has been fantastic.

Live Review: Underworld, DJ Shadow, MSTRKRFT

Blimpy On November - 3 - 2010

Billed as the San Francisco Spookfest and held in the Cow Palace of suburb Daly City, this is one of the most jam packed electronic lineups you’re going to get in North America.  To be able to catch Underworld, DJ Shadow, MSTRKRFT, Booka Shade, Steve Aoki and even Tommy Lee all at once is more than worth the $50 price of admission, and I only saw the first three.  Not wanting to stand on a concrete floor for 8 hours (the show started at 6 PM and ran until 2 AM) and not fuelled by ecstasy like the majority of the scantily clad (both male and female) Halloween partygoers, I don’t regret the decision one bit.

Neon freak out

Wandering into the venue a little after 10, I already knew I was in for a show seeing the characters joining me in the line.  The sheer amount of glow sticks filling the massive venue more than confirmed my suspicion.  The place had an amazing party vibe, and I must say Live Nation ran a hell of a tight show, the only drawback being the $4 bottled water and $8 beer.

MSTRKRFT had the place more than bumpin and I stood thrilled on the concourse taking in the whole scene.  Not realizing it was a full blown stadium complete with your choice of pretty comfortable seats in the stands or being able to walk right up to the stage, I eventually opted for the floor.  The sound was more than adequate, not incredible definition on the high end, but a terrific, pounding sub-bass, about what you’d expect from a stadium built for big sound.

MSTRKRFT rockin the spot

MSTRKRFT lost a bit of momentum as things progressed as they were playing more of a DJ set, descending almost into mashup which was nice to hear a little Daft Punk, but downright upsetting to hear the painfully overused AC/DC.  However, that let down paled in comparison to how the follow-up act DJ Shadow has descended into mail it in territory.

Spending a good 20 minutes setting up what was supposed to be some sort of crazy sphere in the middle of the stage complete with two projectors for 3D effects, it then took him a good 5 minutes of light show build up to break into full song, and it was all downhill from there.  Trying to play to the crowd with more dancey type, big beat music, it was just flashing sphere effects and not memorable at all banging bass for a good 20 minutes without Shadow even showing his face, totally killing the energy of the crowd.  Then, surprise surprise, he popped out from inside the sphere!  Oh my God who would have guessed!

Cow Palace Crowd

Although his salute to the Giants was cute, I’d written Shadow off years ago and this was the last nail in his coffin, I’ll never buy another album or attend another show with the intention of just seeing him.  All he did was close the trap door to the sphere and do the same thing for another 40 minutes.  Songs that were originally prized for their subtlety just had big bass or even poor attempts at dubstep thrown over top, essentially ruining them.  Oh and I was just so surprised he ended with a really short, yet still overblown remix of Organ Donor.  Endtroducing has long since come and gone I’m afraid.

The main act Underworld really can’t do any wrong in my books, I would have paid $50 just to see their hour and a half set again.  Karl Hyde’s energy on the mic and lively dancing hasn’t hardly dropped off at all over the last decade, although mixing board man Rick Smith’s mic check intro of “hello stupid people” certainly showed his age by being both offputting and out of character with the band’s general vibe.  Perhaps this is why some of the younger fans had filtered out or gone to check out the other stage, which I never got a chance to.

While I understand Underworld’s plan in playing three tracks from their new album, with it being such a short set by their standards and the songs not being standouts, it was too bad, but still enjoyable.  I really had it in my head I wanted to hear Push Upstairs, and while that wish wasn’t granted, the mesmerizing everything everything chant and incredible final beat drop of Cowgirl was truly electrifying.  They were able to name one of the best concert DVD’s of all time after that catchphrase, and trust me, it’s that good.

Nearing the end of the show you almost knew King of Snake was coming, and starting it off with a slow build into a truly stunning light show was making me giddy remembering why I had come.  It really was a light show to remember, coupled with a fantastic song and a venue well suiting to both the illuminatory and auditory stimulation that only Underworld can provide.

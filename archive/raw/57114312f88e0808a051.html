Fort George Brewery & Public House

Blimpy On October - 4 - 2010

Having been in Oregon for a grand total of two hours, I was far overdue for a microbrew.  It’s well known there are more of them per capita here than anywhere in the US, so it would be foolish not to see what all the fuss was about.

Kinda hard to miss

Armed with my new GPS powers, and in possession of a killer parking spot, I knew one called Fort George would be right in the path of my wander through Astoria, and happened to share my grandfather’s first name.  Heading along the waterfront until I saw the clearly marked This Way signs, I turned up a block and was quite surprised at how visible, bright, well laid-out and modern the place looked.  For some reason the name, coupled with my preconceived notions of brewpubs had induced visions of musky cedar barrel-lined cellars and the accompanying black stickiness, but that couldn’t have been farther from the truth.

Lots of choices, I'll take one of each

It was Saturday night, the place was hoppin’ but not full, and there were even families with small children (a good state law when you think about it, especially given the economy lately, Recession has been capitalized when I’ve seen it in the paper here).  And not just the patrons, but the huge, multicoloured drink selection chalkboard all screamed out quite festively.  I’d grabbed a paper menu at the door (next to their huge selection of leaflets and posters on local events) and saw there was a taster tray available.  Used to the Brewster’s back home, I anticipated maybe getting to pick out 4 or 5, so was simultaneously scratching my chin, reading the chalkboards and discussing my order with the friendly chap behind the bar when he informed me I got a 4 oz. glass of every beer they made for $12.  Right on, sign me up.

The beers explained

Taking a spot on their large patio with intermittent views of the harbour through warehouses, he brought all twelve of them out on a big ol’ plank of wood, arranged left to right, top to bottom corresponding with the order they were listed on the chalkboards and the laminated beer menu.  Kind of like a book you can drink, he explained, and on my favourite subject no less.  I did mention the bar was already bordering full, but there was only one fellow outside and as he put on a sweater and rubbed his arms he asked me in my t-shirt “aren’t you cold?”  I replied “no, I’m Canadian, this is great!”

I could go through the notes I wrote on a little scratch pad for each glass, but the descriptions on the menu are pretty bang-on so I’ll just do highlights.  The Working Girl Porter was out that day, but was replaced with a comparable Kentucky Coffee Girl, plus a seasonal Hopstoria and a very nifty-premised Co-Hoperative where all the hops were donated from local sources.  Of the whole dozen lot, I was most impressed by the Vortex IPA and Cavatica Stout.  The Vortex really was a swirl of flavour, the inclusion of multiple hops varieties didn’t go unnoticed, and the Cavatica was rich as all hell, almost frightening that its complexities hid the 9% alcohol content, it’s a good thing this isn’t available in Ireland as it reminded me of a thicker Caffrey’s.  I also very much enjoyed the Quick Wit as it wasn’t sweet and had a bit of bite, and I generally can’t stand that style of brew (Noegaarden for Blimpy, ever).

Tastes as good as it looks

Ultimately, it’s quite the testament to a brewery when you can say “yeah, they make a good IPA, stout and Belgian wheat beer”.  This variety alone is worthy of applause, and while several of the less dark beers you could tell had a similar taste, coming from a similar place, this was only an issue when having all twelve at once.  And even still, I would gladly go back and try all twelve all over again.

Taste: 3.5/5

Value: 3.5/5

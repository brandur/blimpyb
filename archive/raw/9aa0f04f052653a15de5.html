The SLO

Blimpy On December - 1 - 2010

That would be San Luis Obispo, pronounced slow.  I never was able to figure out if it was SLO or the SLO.  The first girl I asked was sitting alone at Bull’s Tavern which had attracted me with its $1.50 pints, which I’d soon discover were not full size, and the place was littered with scum bags.  Anyways, this seemingly normal girl, sitting alone at the bar, I ask if she’s waiting for someone to join her or just chillin, she says “just chillin”.  So I continue, “OK then, I’m from Canada just passing through on a road trip, what’s your story?”

Go to the Frog & Peach for this back patio vibe

“Do you even want to know?” she shoots back, pointedly.

“Yeah, I’m just killin time, go for it.”  After an “OK, whatever” I learn in the course of 45 seconds that she was born and raised there, works for Diageo, and at some point when I tell her I actually know that’s the largest liquor distributor in the world and am super interested, she tells me she hates small talk and goes and sidles up with the grimiest motherfucker there.  Point taken, off to the Frog & Peach.

The bartender, she can tell just by the fact I’m there at 7 when it doesn’t start happening until 9 that I’m not from around, we chat while she slices lemons.  They have Murphy’s on tap which I didn’t even know existed outside of Ireland.  There’s no one inside so I head to the back patio because there’s a creek and some foot traffic to the nice restaurants.

The gum wall is quirky and cool, kinda like the town

I’m quickly joined at my picnic bench by three kids my age in the AmeriCorps.  I didn’t know that at the time, but coincidentally asked what AmeriCorps was in passing because I’d seen the word around.  Turns out to essentially be nature conservation work, be it maintaining trails or educating school groups and leading hikes and whatnot.  The catch is, you have to live on a military base, hence the “Corps”.  On a military base you aren’t allowed alcohol or to be intoxicated, even if you’re not in the military.

So these three kids had rented a hotel room for the night to come into town and get rip roaring drunk, and since they seemed to enjoy my merriment and not giving a fuck attitude, I was pulled along for the ride paying for only one of the dozen or so pitchers.  Not actually thinking about it until my stumbling, hiccupping walk back to the RV (no I lie, until the pounding headache the next morning), I haven’t actually been drunk since I left Canada.  I have beers all the time obviously, but not enough to lose my wits.

The old Mission is worth checking out

Perhaps I should lose my wits more often as I certainly had a passably normal conversation with at least 6 different groups of strangers after the AmeriCorps kids had started talking in made up British accents and falling into the flower bushes.  One random fellow told me that when I went to LA if I got lost in the wrong neighbourhood someone would pull me out the car door while upward stabbing through my rib cage into my heart so I would be dead in 10 seconds before I could see them tip my RV over, loot it, then light it on fire.  Perhaps a slightly graphic of a safety reminder that I was already well aware of, but I still don’t understand why they’d tip the RV over, it would be much easier to loot upright.

Another fellow I met was actually named Randy.  I told him he was my hero, that I pretended my name was Randy at restaurants, and probably said “RRRRRRrrrrrandy” about a dozen times.  Then after about a minute of chatting I asked “wait, what was your name again?” and when his response caused flashbacks to such a short period of time earlier, I knew it was probably time to go.

Oh, and I did put up a gallery, and it’s totally kosher to park an RV for a few days in front of the lawyer’s office at the end of Pacific Street past Santa Rosa.

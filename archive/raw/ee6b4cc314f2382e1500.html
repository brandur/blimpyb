Westport and the Docks of the Damned

Blimpy On September - 29 - 2010

I’m not going to ruin the sheer terror of this post with one of the top 10 most beautiful scenes I’ve ever come across (see next post, The Ocean), and don’t get me wrong, I had probably the best 5 days in Seattle humanly possible, but stay away from Westport at night.  And don’t ever park on Dock Street no matter what town you’re in, stereotypes exist for a reason.

Seemed like a good spot at the time

Other than the fact the town was completely dead as I rolled in at dusk after my stunning sightseeing, it seemed like a quiet fishing town.  Problem is, night conceals the fact that it was too quiet.  There’s a sort of peninsula where all the touristy stores and restaurants are, so I thought I’d park there, minutes from the awesome Pacific spectacle I’d just witnessed for a return the following morning.  Finding a stray WiFi signal, I got my internet wits about me for a few hours, then thought I’d take a stroll along the docks to see the fishing boats and smell the salt air.

This was easily achieved in about 10 minutes, enjoying the opportunity to take in my new surroundings.  Unfortunately, these new surroundings had night creatures.  I’m lucky to have fighter pilot vision, and am quite perceptive at times, so it’s probably a good thing I was able to spot a couple places where the fog-shrouded, burnt-out streetlights merged with solitary dark hooded figures in the shadows.  This is why it was only a 10 minute trip, I’ve never seen the movie but I’d rather not meet the Devil’s rejects in the flesh.

Although if I'd seen these everywhere, maybe not such a good spot

Doubling back a different route so as to make it seem like Bessie and I are separate entities, when I get on the street where she’s parked I see a skinny, 5 foot tall, 30 year-old woman walking towards me.  Even in the nicest part of a big city in the middle of the day, for reasons I only loosely understand myself, I generally don’t get into Bessie when there’s somebody right there, it makes me seem a bit like a van-dwelling weirdo.  This certainly being no time for an exception, but as an act of lucky timing, I slow my gait slightly and the woman crosses the street, likely more frightened of me than I of her.  Wanting to hold off entering until she’s a fair distance away, I lean up against the side of Bessie.

The lucky timing turns out to be a ruse.  She wasn’t crossing the street, just walking down the middle of it getting a good look at Bessie’s plates, perhaps wondering what Alberta is, perhaps scoping for an uncurtained window to betray Bessie’s delicious contents in contrast to her poor exterior.  She juts back onto my side of the street, but continues her pace.  I pretend not to notice, pose as sketchily as possible and she walks by.  Phew!

Filet-o-human available upon request

But wait, that would be too easy.  Before getting another 10 feet with me watching from the corner of my eye, she does a quick twirl back my direction, prances over and leans right next to me, our sleeves touching.  When I say twirl and prance, I mean it, and the woman is tiny, so unworried I ask “how’s it going?”  She says nothing, but we are in an affixed gaze, our faces separated by less than a foot.  The eyes as a window to the soul has never been more real than this moment.  Hers are steely and catlike, analyzing as much as she can from my blue as I am her green.

“Umm…so…what’s up?” I try once more.  Silence.  She stays, as does our locked gaze.  After what seems like an eternity, but was likely 5 seconds, she turns and walks in a way that lets me know she’s not leaving just yet, takes another look at the license plate and returns to her perch next to me.  I think even she knew her mime routine on meth was wearing thin at this point, so she unslouched off Bessie, made like she was actually walking off, did a full 360 Gene Kelly sign post twirl to make me think she wasn’t just for an instant, and was gone.

She had cracked just the faintest of smiles when we first met eyes.  Her demeanour was playful the whole time.  But she said nothing.  My heart didn’t start to beat hard until finally inside the RV, fuelled by the horror of her silence in the night.

A shrill whistle outside cut the silence like a guillotine.  Two more in quick succession and I am frozen, contemplating unthinkable possibilities.  Footsteps and three voices directly outside the RV, my teeth clench, my fist tightens, my mind uncontrollably flashes images of the location of the baseball bat, hammer and knives.  One of them touches the RV…baseball bat, hammer, knives…car keys!

No, you won't be seeing me next March

Then nothing else comes of it and they are gone back into the silence.  Car keys.  My house moves, remember?  This scene has played out in my imagination before, believe me, and never until it actually happened did I realize I could just drive off.  And as I did just that under normal circumstances the next morning, I soon noticed the touching of the RV had been one of them deciding to spin my right rear-view mirror the wrong direction, that being the only scathe to both me and Bessie from our haunted port carnival.

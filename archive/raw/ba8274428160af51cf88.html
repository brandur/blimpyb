Gearhart Links Golf Course…and brewpub?

Blimpy On October - 5 - 2010

It’s getting to the point in Oregon I’d like to try stopping in a church or hospital and asking “what have you got on tap?”  Arriving at the course a day early to find out they had no driving range, and that the pro shop guy thought I was completely insane when I asked if I could crash with my RV in the parking lot overnight, I figured I’d check out the Sandtrap pub in the very impressive looking clubhouse.  Surprised at how big and nice it was, and even more surprised still that they too had a beer called Hammerhead, I thought I’d give it a whirl, before learning that this McMenamin’s thing is a 25 location west coast chain and it was in fact the same beer.  Except in small town Oregon it only costs $3.25 during happy hour.

You can even sleep in a van in the parking lot!

Back to golf, the reason I was attracted to this course was its age.  Founded in 1892, it’s a true links style course.  Deep, steep-walled pot bunkers on every hole, thick rough and narrow, undulating fairways.  After I’d proven my sanity to the pro shop guy, he informed me they only installed irrigation in 1999 and that it used to get dry as a bone and brown come August, only adding to the Royal Scottish feel and something I wished I could have tried.

The first tee, and it was all downhill from there

Waiting until the rain subsided at 11 AM the following morning after a successful night in the parking lot (the bus boy at the bar had told me I’d get rustled for sure, so there), I had the course to myself for the winter rate of $35 (it’s $75 at peak summer).  It was in great condition and I was fortunate the rains had softened everything up, it would have been much more difficult otherwise.  But still, not difficult enough.  Not putting 2 and 2 together, if the course really was that old, and right in the middle of town, they wouldn’t have had much room to expand lengthwise with technology over the years.  Kind of like the guy I saw driving through town on a donkey cart.

Great lookin' place

Playing from the back tees, very few par 4s were over 350 yards, if it hadn’t been the first time I’d touched a club in months my score could have been abnormally low.  Any time I actually properly hit a drive, it was an easy wedge into the green, and even a couple birdies.  Naturally these were interspersed with several banana hooks and slices, but even those as long as they didn’t go out of bounds would be in decent shape in the next fairway.

Still, I really liked what the course was going for, the challenge would also have been far greater if the 20 mph gusting winds that appeared the last few holes had been there the whole round.  Another neat feature was the front nine going east-west for head and tailwinds, but the back nine going north-south for crosswinds.  The course had lots of potential for that frustration that comes along with great courses, and while I’d recommend it for the history and great clubhouse at the winter rates, the overall ease of the layout was a bit of a deterrent.

Course: 3/5

Value: 3.5/5

Facilities: 4/5 (Add a driving range and it’d be a 5)

Migration Brewing Co.

Blimpy On October - 11 - 2010

This brewery was literally two blocks away from where I wound up crashing in Portland, so points right off the bat for convenience!  It has a modern neighbourhood pub feel throughout from the shiny aluminum sliding garage door patio to the spacious and stark newly furnished interior.  I say newly furnished because the place actually just opened.  Portland’s newest microbrewery, here I am!

The entrance and patio

Walking up and chatting with the friendly waitress working the bar on Sunday afternoon, I’m fixated as usual on the taps and chalkboard, which is brightly coloured with a selection of the 5 or so in-house brews as new ones are added as they ramp up production.  There are also several guest taps from other Oregon microbreweries, such as the excellent Fort George in Astoria which I already visited.  This is a good sign.

Also a good sign is the availability of their house Clem’s Cream Ale on nitro taps.  A phenomenon I noticed as soon as I crossed into the US, a lot of smaller craft beer places will have one or two nitro taps where the beer is carbonated with nitrogen instead of carbon dioxide, ala Guinness.  It gives the beer a unique creamier texture just like Ireland’s prized stout, so what better to try it on than a cream ale.

A gorgeous pint

When I finally made up my mind on the Clem’s Cream nitro and ordered a pint, the waitress asked surprised “don’t you want to try it first?”  Why as a matter of fact I do, even if just for a free mouthful.  After letting it settle (a humorous site for about an ounce of beer) I find it to be quite tasty and tag on a pulled pork sandwich.  The spectacle of watching the full pint settling is quite the show, so at this point I am psyched to take my seat, read the insanely alternative free local paper and watch some baseball.

This is just plain stupid, but the best way to describe the beer is creamy.  And twofold so if you get it in nitro version.  Definitely a little dance going on in my mouth as I’ve been sticking pretty hard to the IPAs, so the Clem’s Cream gentle, light malty smoothness and no hops kablam or any bitterness was a welcome change.  An excellent beer to just sit and sip, I felt quite distinguished for whatever reason.

The full meal deal

The pulled pork was also tasty, even if it was in a slightly different style.  A nice and fluffy yet still chewy ciabatta held lots of really saucy pork mixed in with coleslaw.  It had a very thrown together feel and wasn’t particularly warmed, but that’s the impression I got looking at their sandwich board and choice to forego a deep-fryer, something seen in more than a few Portland watering holes.  Although with the side of chips and a pickle, it was a bit overpriced at $8.50.

I like this place.  I liked the beer, the food and the service.  But it definitely feels a little bit wet behind the ears, still trying to figure out what it’s going to be after what type of crowd starts gravitating to it.  Too clean, new and nice aren’t usually negatives, and they’re very muted ones at that, but as a writer in the insanely alternative paper described a new rock bar that just opened, it needs a few ripped cushions and some blood stains, if only just to fit in with the other Portland bars.

Taste: 3/5

Value: 2.5/5

San Francisco: You Ain’t Artsier Than Me

Blimpy On November - 15 - 2010

Just as background, that title is reference to the lyrics on the edIT song Artsy Remix.  “You ain’t artsier than me…you’re just a trendy lesbian, dreadlocked thespian” or “You ain’t artsier than me…cuz you shop at Whole Foods in open-toed shoes” and so on.  San Francisco certainly is artsier than me, but they make too much of a point about it.  See the problem is, I like San Fran, but certainly not enough to ever consider living here, and who the hell wants to hear me say nice things, so I’ll just go off for a bit.  I really need to get out of here and head to Santa Cruz.

No clue what this is

Having been in the wilderness and far more liberal places like Arcata for the few weeks before arriving here, I came into town unshaven in cargo pants and sandals, but wanting to jazz myself up threw on my pimp fedora and a fancy looking t-shirt with gold paint.  Right off the bat I got all kinds of looks from people on the street along the lines of “he’s dressing like someone in the too-cool-to-be-cool scene, but…but his outfit doesn’t match and he doesn’t have a haircut that goes with it?!  And those aren’t designer flip flops, they’re plain brown sandals with straps and arch support?!  That’s not cool!”

I’ve never seen so many people with well-kempt dreadlocks.  Isn’t the whole point that you stop washing and let your hair knot itself up so by default they aren’t a fashion statement?   Apparently here they need to be tied in an exquisite bun that matches the hand knitted sweater of the dreadlocked person’s dog or child.

I’ve met plenty of people on my trip who are vegetarians that eat fish from time to time.  They either say “I’m a vegetarian that eats fish from time to time” or “I don’t eat red meat”.  But here they become pescetarians.  Or to fully complete the San Francisco picture, follow up an explanation of what that word is (which I feel is the main reason it’s used in the first place) with “oh and I just hate people having to make a fuss over my diet…but can you try and make tilapia the main course for dinner?”  Maybe I’ll become a vegan that still eats eggs and tell people I’m an ovotarian.

I'm sure that diesel barge is full of sustainable products

The banners on streetlights read “Sustainability, the SF State of Mind.”  Guys, not only does that sound pretentious and is the most overused phrase of the last two years, this is the most rabid consumerist metropolis from here to the Canadian border, there’s an obsession with eating blue fin tuna at the plethora of sushi restaurants.  There are two kinds of people who buy organic mattresses, the rich, and romantic youth who have come here to call themselves pescetarians, then realize they can’t afford the organic mattress they just bought, throw it out and move back home with their parents.  To me sustainability is also related to cost of living and earning a reasonable wage, which is not possible for anyone here but society’s elite.  The entire population of the Oakland-Alameda area I heard referred to as “bridge ‘n tunnelers” many times (next to the phrase “troublemakers”), which I wouldn’t even bother using the “thinly veiled” prefix for describing what it really is.

In the grocery store white asparagus costs the same as regular asparagus, yet I still overheard a woman complaining they only have one type of organic potatoes.  Potatoes grow underground and come covered in dirt that you peel off with the skins, what exactly do you want?  And do you realize how lucky you are to even have a Safeway in the Marina?  I can’t imagine how expensive the property is, if you want to complain go do it at Whole Foods.

At least it's obvious when you're in the Marina district

Oh…and ‘the Marina’, that reminds me of my favourite gripe, the district pissing contest.  The entire city is 7 miles by 7 miles, yet there are apparently 114 different neighbourhoods.  Doing the math that works out to walking half a mile and all of a sudden you’re in a whole new area, even though you can see the one you came from and the buildings and people all look the same.  No one uses landmarks to explain where they live or give directions, it’s all ‘the Mission’ or ‘Lower Haight’ or ‘Russian Hill’, followed by a snobbish scoff if you don’t know where that is, or say “oh, you mean three blocks from here just over that hill?”

To sum it all up, I had a good laugh seeing there was a Cervantes Boulevard because the whole city seems a bit quixotic.  Bam.  At least I got some cool shots in the Gallery.

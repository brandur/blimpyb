Mississippi isn’t backwards, just the politicians

Blimpy On November - 8 - 2011

I have been told for hundreds of miles Mississippi is the most backwards place on the continent, which I have yet to physically see in person after spending the weekend in Jackson.  The history even in Vicksburg (the first town across the Louisiana border, see the gallery here) is absolutely epic and cool, Spanish, French and English settlers, Civil War battles, the first Coca Cola bottling plant, and so on.  However, through some strange twist of irony, I first heard of this crazy idea the second I crossed the border…on today’s state elections ballot will be Initiative 26 to declare a fertilized egg a person, at the instant of conception.

Remember how I said my journey’s a lot of one step backwards, two steps forwards, well this is three steps backwards.

When the levee breaks, momma you got to vote

30% of all females in this country get pregnant by age 20, the highest rate in the industrialized world by more than double second placed England.  And the strange part is people don’t really seem to even equate the two issues, there was a letter to the editor of the Clarion Ledger here in Jackson yesterday morning in favour of abstinence based sex ed which had the golden nugget “With no God, no Bible, no prayer, nothing is sinful, shameful or disgraceful.  Anything goes.”  Because making things sinful and disgraceful obviously discourages people from doing them, rather than making them the forbidden fruit they also coincidentally have never been taught about or exposed to (same goes for binge drinking rates VS Europe where a 13 year old can have wine).  There was also a brilliant quote on Initiative 26 saying “I’ve flipped back and forth on 26, but Supertalk radio put me in the solid yes column.”  Thank God I never turn on the radio.

There is only one abortion provider in the entire state (and 4 Air Force bases) so political motivations are more the gist.  Backed by a group called Parenthood USA that already had the initiative tried and struck down in both Colorado and California, there are several intelligent pro-lifers who point out that the wording of the bill is so strong and flies so in the face of Roe v. Wade, the resulting Supreme Court response after the initiative is challenged could actually strengthen the pro-choice movement, which I would be all for, but we can’t really afford to take the chance.  52 million abortions have been performed since Roe v. Wade, yes a staggering number and not to ballyhoo as a sign of progress, but imagine the state of affairs in modern America if that many unwanted children were around?

...and Justice for all

In another case of wilful ignorance in connecting the dots, the front page of the same paper runs a headline “State Steps Up AIDS Fight” with the sub-caption being through “testing and treatment”, the only approaches the article mentions.  So we’re going to fight an incurable disease with more treatment instead of prevention?

If abortion isn’t fun enough, I can write some stuff about race relations up in the Delta next, but I haven’t been here long enough for legitimate commentary, other than it being pointed out to me that if you think about it, this is actually one of the least segregated places in North America because several races have lived here for hundreds of years and everyone shops at the same stores.  This is quite true, Canada is much more mosaic than melting pot with folks keeping to themselves, I’ll have to mill on it and write more in the future.

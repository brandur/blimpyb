The Prop 19 fallout? None, really.

Blimpy On November - 28 - 2010

For those of you who watched the first episode of Conan’s new show on TBS, his very first guest Seth Rogen summed up the situation in California pretty succinctly, “anyone on Earth can get a medical marijuana card.”  He got his by going into the doctor and explaining “I have a very specific ailment – it’s called, I ain’t got no weed on me right now.”

Real doctors use pot leaf bullet points

I met three perfectly able bodied 20-somethings in San Francisco who had a card due to “back pain”.  Since Arnie pre-emptively passed a decriminalization bill for possession under an ounce, the whole argument almost became irrelevant.  People who want to get high are going to, and it’s really quite harmless on the grand scale of things as long as public perception treats it as such, especially compared to alcohol and tobacco.  Seattle and Portland had already passed civic ballot measures mandating cops treat smoking marijuana as the lowest possible priority offense to arrest people for, below jay walking.

Person of the Year?

What really surprised me was seeing a gigantic joint on the front of Time magazine for a cover story called The United States of Amerijuana.  It might have been nice to publish something before the election, but the fact it’s on there is telling enough.  Whatever sweep it under the rug and pretend it’s not happening part of the issue that has always been present is slowly evaporating.  They go to excruciating lengths in the article to appease their conservative readership base by taking no opinions, drawing no conclusions and extrapolating next to nothing enlightening, but the fact it’s on the cover alone makes me glad I have a copy as a keepsake.  It’s almost like “I was in America the first time a giant joint was on the cover of Time magazine!”  Small victories, y’know?  Of course this is the same Time magazine that has LeBron James on the shortlist for Person of the Year in 2010, but lambasting their journalistic decline isn’t the point here.  Wait…they should make the giant joint Person of the Year!

So where do we go from here?  I think wait and see is the best answer.  The article somehow links the idea of attacking the winnable battle of medical marijuana first and letting it slide from there to George Soros, and while I don’t think he’s solely the one behind it, the concept is working.  In the Prop 19 polls the only two age groups decidedly in the NO were seniors and surprisingly Generation X, being the ones most likely to have teenage children susceptible to the devil weed.  But it wouldn’t be a hard argument to make that the internet is just as powerful of a mind altering drug.  If we just wait for everyone to get a bit older and a bit more open minded, legalization might become a reality.

If Raekwon can, so can you

Not that I even think it needs to be or care anymore, which was my point in the beginning.  Where I found in Vancouver there was a massive pot culture, increasingly more in Seattle, then Portland, then especially San Francisco, there were skunky clouds wafting through the air because people just didn’t give a shit.  I quit picking up weed sacks after crossing the border out of what I thought was sound self-preservation from a criminal standpoint, but I still wind up randomly getting high a couple times a week, it just doesn’t matter anywhere on the West coast.  Naturally I’m not expecting this to hold true in Missouri, but as with the debate itself, only time will tell.

Roll it up, light it up, vote it up?  Whatever, maaan.

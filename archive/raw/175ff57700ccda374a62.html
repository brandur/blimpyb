Album of the Week: Shades of Blue by Madlib

Blimpy On September - 11 - 2010

Take one of the best and most prolific hip hop producers of the past decade, give him exclusive access to all the original archives of the Blue Note jazz label and this is what you get.  I’ve probably listened to this album a hundred times and still couldn’t tell you if it’s jazz or hip hop, which I think is testament in itself.  It’s seminal, that’s all I can say.

Hailing from Oxnard California, Madlib and his many incarnations have been a prominent force for the last decade.  Either in collaborations with Jay Dilla or MF Doom (under the monikers Jaylib and Madvillain) or on his own as the Beat Konducta, Yesterday’s New Quintet or just plain old Madlib, he’s been putting out multiple albums a year for the last several.  While some would see this as dilution, I see it as a testament to a guy with a lot of ideas.

Backtracking to 2002 when Shades of Blue was released, we find an up-and-coming Madlib somehow pitching the most brilliant of ideas to the Blue Note label, then delivering.  Since its founding in the late 1930s, Blue Note has put out releases from Thelonius Monk, Miles Davis, John Coltrane, Jimmy Smith, Cannonball Adderley and many more.  This is what you would call ‘good source material’.  But that’s only a small part of the battle, as is the mark of any producer of his calibre Madlib is able to take all these classic samples from decades ago and craft them to fit his own modern hip hop sound.

The result?  Smooth like butter and definitely groovin.  Jazz flute, meet gangsta.

I’m just going to come flat out and say it, but probably the strongest ties between the two different genres are drum breakdowns and marijuana use, so I’m in seventh heaven.  And that thread is heavily emphasized in Madlib’s fusion sound.  There’s a very strong muted (or blunted) chill vibe where the jazz melodies are held up by a drum sample, but still allowed to stand on their own musicality.  In his later work Madlib often resorts to 2 minutes songs as showcases for his nifty hooks, but on Shades of Blue most of the tracks clock in at over 4 minutes to allow the original jazz tunes to play out to their fullest, while building on top of Madlib’s funky remixed backbeats.

Another highlight of the album is Madlib’s reverence of the source material, which is readily apparent in the sound coming from every song on the record.  Equally entertaining is the use of vocal samples to further emphasize this.  Whether it’s digging up historical sound bites from the Blue Note archives, or getting fresh reactions from artists today on what Madlib is doing, interspersing these throughout the record helps tell a bit of a story and add some more humour to an already fun record.  Apparently the third common thread between classic jazz and hip hop is the tongue-in-cheek reference to artist’s zodiac signs.

Basically, if you’re looking to throw on some tunes that will agree with most everyone while still being original and fresh (and having a good back story), look no further than the excellent Shades of Blue.

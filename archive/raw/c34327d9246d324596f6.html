The Delta Diaries: Travelling Riverside Blues

Blimpy On November - 24 - 2011

Friar’s point, Vicksburg, Rosedale, at least I’ve been to all the places Robert Johnson mentions in the song.  Nobody’s squeezed my lemon ‘til the juice runs down my leg yet…not that I have any clue what that’s supposed to mean…

R.I.P. King of the Delta Blues

Travelling back in time to the pilgrimage to his gravesite a few weeks ago (at least I think, time and space get thicker and muddled in the Delta) it was both a letdown and better than I hoped, as I’ve found with anywhere I have expectations about.  The number one problem was the dispute about where he was actually buried, there are three places in the vicinity of Greenwood Mississippi at one point laying claim, all still bearing grave markers.  Same goes for which jook joint he got poisoned at, or which plantation he crawled off to die at.  Even though the Little Zion M.B. Church I visited is the most believed burial ground, the marker there was placed in 2000 and is certainly not over top of Mr. Johnson’s body.

This is a shame because I had hoped to snag a blade of grass with some of his carbon atoms to put in a little pouch as part of my mojo hand, but I think I’m resigned to having my mojo hand just be in my head cuz I ain’t superstitious.  What’s also too bad is the marker was placed there by a fellow named Stephen LaVere, a local “historian” who used to run a Robert Johnson museum in town, which I read several reviews of it actually being a museum to himself.  Turns out he was just saving money to move to Hawaii and really had no allegiance to Greenwood at all, there’s still a note posted on the door offering to sell his Robert Johnson memorabilia collection as a whole or piecemeal, whichever nets the highest bid.  And honestly, who the hell puts their name on someone else’s grave marker?

I hung out here for at least an hour, amazingly tranquil

Spending this much time in the Delta is starting to give me my own travelling riverside blues, I’m a directionless fish out of water.  I don’t translate to people, nor them to me.  I tell my story, people think it’s ‘neat’, then within two minutes they are giving me their slant about all kinds of racist leanings they have, but telling me it’s “OK” because this is Mississippi, it’s honestly happened time and again.  There are still black and white high schools in most towns, I get that, it’s for complex historical reasons (called slavery, which you aren’t allowed to talk about), but once the conversation starts swaying to your grandmother not being able to get welfare because she’s white and we have a black president…wait, what?  You lost me.

It also definitely makes me realize that while I love Delta blues music, it doesn’t belong to me in any way shape or form, never will.  If this is what it’s like now, the 1930’s are surely unimaginable to my white Canadian brain.  I mean black people were still getting murdered in the 1960’s just for trying to register to vote.  And while I was originally asking a lot of people why there wasn’t more tourism in this area because it’s gorgeous, it’s still…I dunno…the Delta.  There’s something impenetrably thick about the place if you weren’t born and raised here, just walking down the street all kinds of folk (white folk, actually) stare at me up and down disdainfully for several seconds because I’m clearly not from around here.  Nobody wears sunglasses, nobody has long hair, that’s just the way it goes, and I’m more than willing to accept that acceptance doesn’t go both ways.

Uhhhh…happy Thanksgiving y’all!

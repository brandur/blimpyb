The Carmel Safeway

Blimpy On November - 27 - 2010

Yes that’s right, Safeway the grocery store.  I’ve had all kinds of grocery store observations brewing in my head since crossing into America but this particular location was exceptional enough to warrant its own topic without even mentioning all my past food shopping musings.

For those of you who haven’t heard of it, Carmel is perhaps one of poshest places on the entire West coast, I didn’t even bother stopping in out of fear and distain.  Pebble Beach Golf Course resides there, there is a scenic 17 Mile Drive through town with an $8 fee just to have the pleasure of admiring rich people’s homes, they don’t allow chain stores within the town limits, there aren’t any streetlights and Clint Eastwood used to be mayor.  OK, that last one’s more of a plus.

Anybody want a soda pop?

Anyways, factoring in the chain store backlash, it makes perfect sense there’d be a Safeway just across the highway from town and it would be an absolute free for all.  I was told of it twice in Santa Cruz as a ‘must stop’ before I hit the wilderness in Big Sur, and now I see why.  Before I delve into the details, Canadians be warned, we get ripped off absolutely heinously on most everything we purchase at the exact same Safeway chain either through shipping costs, taxes or probably most strongly a lack of true American cutthroat competition.  Oh, and my Safeway club membership works here too using my parent’s phone number, I’m throwing a market research guy for a loop somewhere no doubt.

My version of "roughing it"

Having run out of Diet Coke (I like to have a can in the afternoon every now and again, though not nearly as frequently as when they were free at my old office) I went to that aisle first to grab a 12-case.  Literally doing a triple take, it was buy 2 get 3 free of any flavour of any brand.  So for $12 total, I got a 60 can sampler of Diet Coke, Coke Zero, Ginger Ale, Fresca and Fanta.  Yeah, it’s overkill, but 20 cents a can!!  I also have a major problem with stuff rattling around in the fridge as I drive, now I can just completely pack it with pop cans, and it won’t lose as much cold when I open the door!

So now that I’ve got all this mix, and having not drank any hard alcohol in about a month, I thought maybe some whiskey or rum might be a nice addition.  I was kind of upset Captain Morgan and Wild Turkey 26’s (or a fifth in the USA) ran for about $17, which is only maybe 30% cheaper than Canada, but keeping with the Canadian theme I came across a 60 ounce bottle of some rye called Canadian Crest for $12!  This must be the magic multiplier of the day, that’s 20 cents a shot!  Who cares that I’m Canadian and have never even heard of it!

Amazing leftovers

Wanting to get some meat for campfire pit roasting, I was originally thinking of pork shoulder to try something new, but when I took a look at the deals in the beef section I couldn’t say no.  A 3 lb. rib eye roast was only $15, you could slice it in 3 and have a trio of beautiful 16 oz steaks for Pete’s sake.

Deals aside, I still think the highlight was watching one of the produce guys accidentally knock his cart into a tower of glass balsamic vinegar bottles and shatter dozens on the floor.  Well, that wasn’t the highlight, I’m not that mean, but the 6 year old too young to understand such cruelty who immediately walked over and said “why did you do that?  You made a big mess.” was pretty good, as was the lingering smell throughout the produce section and hearing done-up women with their leather handbags and feather hats proclaiming “my the vegetables smell off today.”

How to win $50 without having a clue

Blimpy On October - 13 - 2010

My first casino parking lot stay at the Three Rivers Casino & Hotel just a couple miles outside Florence, Oregon was a real head scratcher.  I did a truck stop outside Eugene for the first time last night, so figured I’d keep the string of obscure, complimentary lodgings going.

No pictures allowed inside

As usual, not thinking ahead to brace myself after the absolutely stunning hour drive through the hills, the place is massive enough to warrant a half mile of parking lot with a separate tent for the bingo parlour.  Seeing a dedicated RV Overnight Parking for the first time ever, I read the sign’s fine print that I need to get a pass from security.  I chatted with the fellow at the desk and he says no money down, and I can stay for 7 days if I’d like.  No thanks, one day in hell is enough.

The place really is hell, Satan couldn’t find anywhere more fitting to stab me with his pitchfork for eternity.  It’s all very nice and shiny, but too much so.  I’m the youngest person here by 20 years.  Watching people puff on their cigarettes inside a closed building is trip enough, but watching the smoke instantly disappear into the rafters reminds me of the old adding oxygen to the ventilation trick to keep people more awake.  I don’t even know if this is true, but thinking about it makes my pupils dilate ever so slightly.

Rather than having a sandwich for dinner for the third day running, I decide to see if their buffet is acceptable.  It’s a pretty darn good spread and the $14.99 cost includes $4 in slot machine credits.  I was already going to blow $5 just to say I did, so I grab my book and return to gorge myself.  Prime rib, salmon, pork chops, pesto pasta, stir fry, mashed potatoes, green beans, broccoli, spring rolls, fried rice, sundae bar…this is just the list of things I had, all in a bite-sized, exceptionally mediocre splendour.  Still in awe staring at the whole spectacle, and thoroughly enjoying the concept of every bite more than the bite itself, coupled with the side-glances of retirees and degenerate gamblers, I felt like Jean-Claude Van Damme in the diner scene from Universal Soldier, minus the roundhouse kicks.

Ugly place

Going up to the Players Club desk for my $4 credit as the ticket instructed me, I discover I need to register for said Players Club, which is free.  I give them a drivers license that has an address I don’t live at, am told they’ve added $10 just for me signing up, they give me one of those swipe cards only problem gamblers have (I declined the lanyard) and two pages of instructions on how to actually use my free money.  Apparently I still have to pay to gamble, but the machine reimburses me?

This being my third time ever in a casino, I remember from the second that it’s best to find a slot machine and play max credits.  You see, the penny slots allow you to play 15 pennies at once and this increases your odds, it’s a good little trick.  Not wanting to ruin the cheap thrill all at once, I find a 25 cent machine that allows 3 credits max and slide in a dollar bill.  Three spinning wheels, one horizontal line, bar, cherry, bell and 7, no Sex In The City or Amazing Race slot machines for me.  Except this machine has a bonus 4th wheel for the max credit folks like myself.

Spin one, nothing, but the $14 tally declines to $13.25 and I still have 3 credits.  OK gotcha.  Spin two, some sort of royal sampler, I’m at 8 credits or something.  Spin three, green 7, green 7, dammit nothing.  But the 4th magic wheel says something too quickly for me to catch, the dammit nothing is spun to a green 7 and I have over 100 credits.  I quickly jam Cash Out, run to the teller and ask her if this is real money.  She counts out $29.25 laughing, touching the real bills reassures me.  Now that I sort of get the idea I return to the same machine and quickly deplete my free money, until on the last spin the 4th magic wheel somehow causes all the original wheels to spin multiple directions many times until I have some sort of combination of bars, another $21.50 and a quick exit from hell.

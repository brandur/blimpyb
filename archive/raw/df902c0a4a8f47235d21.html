Occupy Dallas

Blimpy On October - 27 - 2011

This revolution will not be televised…because it’s not a revolution.

Don’t get me wrong, I’m sympathetic to the cause.  Wealth disparity in this country is massive (as bad as it’s been since 1928), real income has declined substantially in the last decade, special interest corporate money does buy politicians…but I violated my cardinal rule by researching a place too long before I went there via the Occupy Dallas facebook page.  Two days ago 24 members were arrested for linking arms in front of a bank downtown and stopping normal people from entering while ragging on them to close their accounts.  Umm, that’s a crime, it’s called disturbing the peace, harassment, what have you.  What if all the Texas Republicans who disagree with #Occupy linked arms and prevented the tent city members from getting back to their shanty town by City Hall?

Say what you will, but I've never been arrested for no reason

So on their facebook was a “desperate plea” for information regarding the harassment of these prisoners and them not being given access to water, i.e. a “desperate plea” for attention.  Well, around the 5th comment was someone saying “all the prisoners with a fixed address and no criminal record have already been released, and they only had bottled water withheld, there’s a fountain in every cell.”  Yikes, that was actually the truth, rail against the news media all you want, but a half-truth is just that.  We’re talking about jail here for breaking a legitimate law, 30 years ago you would have got the phone book treatment in a heartbeat, and if I’m in there for stabbing a guy over a vial of crack, damn right I’m going to be harassing you.

Oh, and on the occupydallas.org site under the Donations section they are asking for a larger coffee maker.  To the next person I hear comparing #Occupy crackdowns with Tahir Square, I would be happy to go Mubarek on them, which would entail the internet being shut down and them dying.  We get to vote, we get lawyers in jail.  And yeah, I heard about Oakland, but that place is a different animal, there was a larger riot when I was there last year and you’ll notice all the news articles describe the protestors as “for the most part peaceful”.  Well, one bad apple I’m afraid folks.  The main thing it proves to me is the #Occupy movement’s best chance comes through the authorities acting foolishly, not them acting wisely.

I'm at least behind that

Arriving at the Dallas site the next morning, my honest initial reaction was “this is sad”.  Walking up I had a good angle with the tents in the background and a half dozen cops just standing and chatting (who I learned were in fact there to deal with the numerous nightly reports of the protestors robbing each other…ironic, I know.)  As I was snapping the shot I heard one protester asking another where she was scuttling off to, having not noticed them standing to my left in the slightest, to which she cast a fierce glance in my direction and said “this guy’s trying to take my picture.”  If you’d been involved in the movement the several weeks it’s been happening, surely countless other photographers and news media who don’t have footlong hair and haven’t bathed in a week have spotted you to put on their US government/Google conspiracy hit list.  And shouldn’t you be trying to get me to put a positive spin on this rather than being hostile and ashamed?  Oh, and last but not least, you weren’t even in the shot, bitch.

During my hour hanging around camp I had 2 protesters ask me for spare change to buy a Dr. Pepper (it was invented here, there’s a local version made with pure cane sugar, it’s a fair request) and 3 normal civilians ask me why there were tents everywhere.  I told one it was an offshoot of Occupy Wall Street to which she replied “what’s that?” so it’s equally sad on both sides.  While I was dropping off a jug of water and a spare tarp I brought, I asked a fellow if they had any actions planned today, and he told me “oh, we’ll march somewhere.”  Exactly.

10% of the 99% think this is a joke

The one saving grace of the whole affair was a legit homeless black guy named Jay who I spoke with for a good 10 minutes.  He had things to say about unemployment in Spain and the globalized economy being intertwined, that the US dollar was nothing but a promissory note, that he’d had several jobs where his employer would only ever give him 24 hours a week, whatever the magic number is to not have to give benefits.  That was a great conversation.  Oh, and there were some good signs posted around the camp.  And yes, the system is broken and this raising awareness can’t hurt, but there’s something eerily off about what’s happening there considering I had every intention of staying at least one night and almost immediately abandoned such a notion.  Taking a look at their poster Occupations of our Occupiers, people had written down “Philosopher, Revolutionary, Leader, Creative Person, Graffiti Student and Pimp”.  None of those are occupations and are case in point why movements like We Are The 53% (who pay income tax and don’t sympathize) have sprung up.

Feeling that I needed to cleanse myself by buying some Starbucks and throwing out half of it just because I can, I headed towards the JFK Plaza.  Oswald was a pretty good shot!  And so was J. Edgar Hoover dressed in drag as the second shooter on the grassy knoll, or however the guy selling leaflets on the corner tells it.  But on my return pass by the Occupy camp to where I parked, sure enough there was a shirtless guy with a shaved head but for a single dreadlock down to his waist playing hula hoop in the middle of the street quite obviously high as the goddam sky and the cops laughing their asses off in the background.  And I walked right past laughing with them.

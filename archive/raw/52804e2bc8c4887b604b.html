Giordano Brothers

Blimpy On November - 11 - 2010

Transplanted from Pittsburgh to the edge of Chinatown in San Francisco, afternoon, evening or night Giordano Brothers is the most “can’t miss” bar I’ve come across in the last few thousand miles.

#8 is the key point

First things first, their signature sandwich.  White bread, meat of your choice, coleslaw and French fries, these are all the ingredients in the sandwich and the taste is as ridiculously over the top as it sounds.  To make matters even better, try asking them to change it because you don’t want fries in your sandwich and you will likely be told to shut up and leave.  Just read the sign if you don’t believe me.

I had to randomly wander by the place a second time to try their signature coppa sandwich because the first time I was there I got too drunk to remember to order food.  Settling in with it much emptier in the middle of the afternoon, a few regulars popped in and out, I got a chance to take in how much Steelers gear there is on the wall, how sticky the floor and tables are and how generally awesome the place is, totally stained with character.

Tastes as wild as it looks

Coppa is similar to prosciutto, with a little bit more meat and less fat but definitely salty, which makes a good base layer for what turns out to be a damn fine sandwich.  I ordered the thing only because it sounds so silly, but it’s built that way for a reason.  Their coleslaw is advertized throughout the place as “oil and vinegar coleslaw” and it really is that, not creamy, just a light shredded cabbage salad with zing.  Pair that with fries to soak up more of the flavour and give it a bit of crunch, it really is a unique creation you’d be foolish to pass up.

Plenty of food and drink selection

Rewinding to my first stop at the joint was a completely different experience.  We couldn’t find the hockey game on anywhere on a Saturday night just after the Giants game and with college football on, so we popped in and the buddy I was with was a regular and knew the bartender John who turns out knew more about old school hockey than I did and was glad to switch one of the TVs after we grabbed the last table.  John was one of the funnier and livelier guys I’ve met in awhile and in between making jokes about telling the people who just sat down on the patio it was closed (for fun because they were dressed too European to be at a bar like that) and that he hated Asian people (he was Asian), he was more than happy to stock us up with $2 bottles of Session by the armful and a damn fine order of wings too.

You had me at $2 beers

After a few hours in the place, they started clearing out a couple tables in the corner and some grizzled fellows with guitar cases started rolling in amps.  “Oh no” I thought to myself as I don’t like bar bands blaring 3 feet away from me, but I was dead wrong and glad I stuck around.  The lead singer and guitarist had a cheesy half British accent to match his half unbuttoned shirt and greasy black hair.  He was the show, I don’t even remember the drummer or bassist, but I suppose that’s understandable after he took my beer still with a couple sips in it to use as a slide while standing on the bar, then did a flip trick with the bottle not realizing, spraying himself and some patrons only to keep on rocking.  The sound matched too, beer-soaked Stevie Ray and Jimi jams with plenty of guts and fierce licks that definitely made me feel like I was in a Pittsburgh blues rock bar…because I was, it just happens to be on the West Coast.

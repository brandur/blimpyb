Phoenix NASCAR Subway 500

Blimpy On March - 2 - 2011

Nothing’s more American than NASCAR, beer and hot dogs.  Rolling into Phoenix for the Subway Fresh Fit 500, I met up with one of my many connects across the country and managed to snag a $400 all access pit hot pass.  So as you can see from the gallery, I was able to get up close and personal with the cars in the garage, stand on pit road during the race and pretend I knew what I was doing to get into the professional photographer only area on turn 1 for some great shots during the race.  It was a little daunting at first trying to not get run over or get in the way of pit crew folks rolling around tires and fuel canisters, but I got the hang of it pretty quick and didn’t get yelled at once all weekend!

Right there in the pits

Also, for you folks reading at home, I changed the site a little bit so you can click on one thumbnail to get the blown up version, then just cycle through all of them, should make it a little easier to navigate.  It’s something called lightbox, and even facebook switched to using it recently, so I may as well get on board.

While watching souped up cars driving around in circles isn’t really my thing, it’s certainly something worth doing once, if only just to observe your typical NASCAR fan.  Being in the pits I got more of the white trash with money crowd, but I was still able to see a missing teeth, camo wearing bearded fat guy yelling at his children while swilling a beer and overhear someone criticizing the pole sitter of the race for being an atheist.  Rookie Trevor Bayne who won the Daytona 500 the week prior is a bible thumper, so huge with the fans, and the media was all over his jock all week.  He’s only 20 so I’ll root for the kid too, but after wrecking in practice he managed the same feat during the race only 20 laps in.

Zoom zoom

The race itself was a giant mess, only half the cars in the field finished, there was even a full blown red flag where everyone had to just stop in the middle of the track to clean up all the smashed car bits.  In the end Jeff Gordon wound up winning his first race in over 2 years, and you could tell all day his car was just faster.  Hanging out with the race engineers for the RCR team (Richard Childress Racing with drivers Jeff Burton, Kevin Harvick and Paul Menard) I learned that while the good ol’ boy mentality of the sport still prevails, they’re slowly switching over to a more scientific perspective.  A lot of talk of wing, camber, spring stiffness and tire pressure was over my head, but it’s neat to see the delicate balance and learn that the engineers job isn’t just to make the car as fast as a 3D computer simulation will allow on a given track, they actually described that as only 40% of the battle, the other 60% being to listen to the driver and get the car to respond to his particular racing style.

Hehehe

While many of the drivers are derided as prima donnas who aren’t held accountable, I got to meet Paul Menard and he was a nice guy who actually introduced himself to me and shook my hand.  I suppose I was in his trailer and maybe he wanted to be nice so I wouldn’t touch anything, but I hear he’s a down to earth guy.

A lot of the real magic is in the sound and motion, and I have about 30 different clips of footage I need to edit together (ohh right, the Youtube from the pits!), but that’s not exactly a priority right now and having never edited a video before, perhaps not the best use of my time when I’m getting ready to drive back to Quartzsite again to sit in the middle of the desert.  In the meantime, check the gallery.

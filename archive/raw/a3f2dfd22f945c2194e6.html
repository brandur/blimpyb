Thin Crust Pizza

Blimpy On August - 31 - 2010

The second promised tomato sauce leftover recipe, this one is dead simple and a hell of a lot cheaper, better and better for you than Dominos or Pizza Hut.  The ingredients on the pizza are solely up to you and there are infinite varieties, so I’ll just tell it how I made it, you can tweak as you wish.

Serves 2

Ingredients

  • 1 can Pillsbury pizza dough

    Can't make good food without good ingredients

  • 1 cup tomato sauce
  • 1 tomato, thinly sliced
  • 2 white mushrooms, thinly sliced
  • 1 roasted Anaheim pepper, thinly sliced
  • 1 leftover eggplant parmesan slice, chopped
  • 12 thin slices of salami
  • 6 leaves of fresh basil
  • 1 cup grated mozzarella cheese
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Dried chilli flakes

Preparation

Other than the pizza dough, this dish was made entirely of leftovers from the creation of the sauce and the parm.  Hell, you could even make the dough yourself if you got flour kicking around, it just happens to not be my thing and the Pillsbury can only costs $3.

Not my best work, just so long as there aren't holes in it

Following the directions on the pizza dough package, preheat the oven to 400 degrees F.  Take out a pizza pan (or baking sheet if that’s all you got) and either flour or oil the bottom so the dough doesn’t stick.  If you want true thin crust, only use half of the pizza dough, or use the whole thing for regular or deep dish variety.  Pressing it out flat with your fingers will be real hard to do without ripping the dough, so I recommend a rolling pin, or just improvise and use a wine bottle like I did.  Oil or flour the rolling pin/wine bottle and gradually press the dough out so it fills the pan to the edge and is the desired thickness.

The dough package says put it in the oven for 8 minutes to allow rising, I find no more than 6 minutes if you have thin crust, so keep an eye on it and if you see any browning, immediately take it out.  There will be some bubbles and air pockets in the dough, they will generally settle themselves out, but you can poke them with a knife to make sure they’re gone as well.

While the dough is rising, slice up all your toppings.  An expert tip I learned from my dear old mother is for any particularly liquidy toppings (tomato, roasted pepper) cut them up first and lay them out on paper towel to absorb the juices.  This will prevent the pizza from getting soggy and keep it crispy, as well as alleviating the feeling of boiling tomatoes burning the roof of your mouth on the first bite.

Add cheese. Fire it up.

Some would argue differently, but I like to layer my toppings in a particular order on the pizza.  Basil goes on first, followed by tomato, then mushroom, just like a marguerita pizza.  Then go with any of the roasted vegetables.  I like to put the meat on top so it is most exposed to the heat to allow its delicious juices to sizzle up and escape.  Then sprinkle on any spices (parsley or parmesan are both good ones I haven’t listed).

For the cheese, if you have trouble grating it because it’s too soft, just pop it in the freezer for 10 minutes and problem solved.  Evenly distribute the cheese to lightly cover all the toppings.

Cooking

Raise the oven temperature to 450 degrees F.  Once at temperature, slide the pizza pan in and leave for about 6 to 8 minutes before checking on it.  At the point when the cheese is all melted and starting to bubble, but before it is brown, switch the oven to broil.  Leaving it like this for the last 2 minutes will nicely brown the top and crisp everything up.

Remember to let it cool, remember to let it cool...

Remove the pizza from the oven when the cheese and crust are browned but not burnt and let sit for at least 5 minutes.  I’ve burned myself more times than I can count wanting the delicious flavour in my mouth too soon.  Once cooled enough, slice in quarters and enjoy with your favourite hot sauce or ranch to dip the crusts in.  Savour.

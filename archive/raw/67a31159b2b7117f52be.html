City View Storage

Blimpy On November - 17 - 2010

OK, parking isn’t exactly thrilling (although Treasure Island’s story contained in this post is), this is just a recommendation to fellow boondockers, or anyone driving through San Francisco looking for a place to put their motor vehicle.  Probably one of the best spots you’re going to find is City View Storage on Treasure Island.

The east view from Treasure Island

As I said, Treasure Island is a good story in and of itself.  Attached to Yerba Buena Island at the midpoint of the Bay Bridge connecting Oakland and San Francisco, Treasure Island is made up of leftover sea bottom dredge from building the Golden Gate Bridge and was constructed for the 1939 World’s Fair.  It’s flat as a board, no more than 10 feet above sea level at any point and having pretty nice views and being fairly large is a favourite destination of cyclists and joggers looking for an afternoon jaunt around movie production studios and low income housing.  It used to be a military base that has since been decommissioned, and now the bus runs 24/7 every 30 minutes right to the main terminal downtown making it easy to get to and from.

The west view from Treasure Island

Being San Fran, there are a few 21st century proposals out there to turn Treasure Island into a car-free, green, sustainable living community sometime in the near future, although with no way to or from other than the traffic-horrendous Bay Bridge (which has no pedestrian walkway), I’m not exactly sure how that’s supposed to work.  Coupled with the fact that the soil is so rich in radium and plutonium the EPA recommends against living there and the place will liquefy during an earthquake, it might be just another green daydream, but there are lots of palm trees!

Movie prop or radiation victim?

Back to City View Storage, I found the place online and was pleased to learn their RV rate is a mere $5 per foot per month.  As I was planning on crashing at a friend’s place and didn’t want to burden Bessie with hilly city driving, I could have left her there for a week and still been happy to only pay $115 to park an RV that close to San Fran.  In the end I left her there for 3 weeks in a razor wire fenced lot with a night watchman and had everything in perfect working order on my return.  Well, almost everything, let’s not count the emotional scarring involved in abandoning an RV you’ve lived in for 5 months straight.  I’m sorry girl, I’ll never do that again.

The lot is run by a young Scottish fellow my age who was more than accommodating, seemingly instantly replying to any e-mail or text I sent with questions or coordinating meeting up at the lot, and was even coincidentally there when I left to give a friendly goodbye.  And Bessie ran like a dream for the 100 miles I drove that day after three weeks of inactivity, which I attribute in no small part to Treasure Island’s radioactive soil.

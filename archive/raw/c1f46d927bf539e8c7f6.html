Live Review: Starkey & Ikonika at The Baltic Room

Blimpy On September - 25 - 2010

Billed as the Planet Mu Showcase of Seattle’s Decibel Festival, The Baltic Room played host to the dubstep and bass stylings of FaultyDL, Ikonika and Starkey.  Naturally I thought this would be the biggest show in the entire world as the Planet Mu label’s founder Mike Paradinas is one of my favourite artists out there, and both Starkey and Ikonika have a truly unique sound.  However, the $15 price of admission made me realize that the other festival Friday night main event of Modeselektor was perhaps the bigger draw.  This is in no way a bad thing, only 300 people at the venue and room to breathe and move are sometimes nice.

The Baltic Room however, not so nice.  Well, in terms of a place to go have a drink, it would be more than adequate.  21st century bohemian decor, orange and blue lighting, two floors with seats nestled in the corner and two accessible bars, with excellent beer on tap selection, friendly bouncers, sounds like it could be worse.  When I knock the venue, I’m only knocking one aspect of it and that was the sound system.  Terrible!  The worst sound I’ve ever heard for any show.  I’ll touch on this more, but I had a real problem standing there listening and thinking “my RV sounds a LOT better than this!”

On the positive tip, I was borderline blown away by Ikonika’s live set.  I’ve listened to her LP Contact, Want, Love, Have a few times, but it never really got hold of me.  Within minutes of wandering in around 11 for the start of her set though, I was hooked.  Much more stripped down, much more primal and flawlessly mixed.  I was immediately brought back to memories of Ellen Allien at 4 AM in Madrid a few years back, naturally partly because you don’t find many female hard electro DJ’s, but there was more to it than that.  Ikonika is perhaps three quarters of the way there, and at least a dozen beats drops during the set elicited an impulse response from the crowd to just move.  I even overheard from the people beside me “I’ve never seen people move like this at a Seattle electro show!”

During her set, I wasn’t too bothered by the shortcomings of the sound, but was pretty aware of lacking in the low lows and the high highs.  In hindsight, I think a lot of this had to do with Ikonika’s DJing and knob-twiddling skills.  I saw her constantly tweaking the boards, and I honestly believe she was playing to the room itself by EQ’ing to avoid dead zones and still keep the sound live.  Or maybe it was just coincidence.

By the time Starkey had sidled up to the booth and Ikonika was winding down, the crowd was swept with a hushed awe as the two switched laptop connections.  He eased into it with a couple knob-twiddling tricks and some cuts from his Street Bass Anthems mixes, before dropping a new beat that definitely rocked the spot.  But something was missing.  No true low end, uneven sound in different spots of the club, all becoming painfully clear watching the sound tech guys turning speakers around so they could hit the reset switches, basically doing nothing but praying they would stop overheating and start working properly.  And it only got worse from here.

I can’t describe how distracting and painful it was watching Starkey have to mix from one cut to another, then spin his own monitor around and reset it, after pleading with the sound techs to fix it and their solution being waving drink coasters at the back port of the speakers.  Even worse was this making me realize exactly why I love listening to Starkey on my own system.  It’s very kitschy, centered on highlighting extreme frequencies, something like auditory porn.  But going to see it in this live setting was like the time I was at a strip club and the girl on stage hadn’t spent enough on her boob job and one was noticeably way bigger than the other, to the point it completely distracted from the show.

Don’t get me wrong, it was still totally worth $15 to see two artists I respect a lot, and even though there wasn’t much variation in Starkey’s cuts from listening to the album version, watching it come from his fingers live is always a thrill, it just would have been all the more thrilling at an actual concert venue.

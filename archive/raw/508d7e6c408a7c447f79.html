Album of the Week: Bikini Bandits, Kill! Kill! Kill! by Bong-Ra

Blimpy On September - 20 - 2010

I could pick this album because of the cover art alone.  It’s slick as all hell, posturingly aggressive and still ridiculous enough to have you asking yourself “really?”.  And it’s detailed enough that there’s a picnic and BBQ in the background.  Funnily enough, that all captures the aesthetic of the music itself.

Just so you’re forewarned, this album is hard, fast, loud and busy electronic music.  I’d call it breakcore, it’s basically drum-n-bass on steroids but tinged with hip hop and ragga to give it a flavour that’s Bong-Ra’s own.  Before you let that scare you off though, the man is an absolute master of atmosphere and this is one of the shimmering standouts of the genre.

Hailing from Holland, Bong-Ra has been fairly prominent on the European hardcore scene for the last decade.  He’s one of those saddling the line between genius and madness guys ala Venetian Snares or Aphex Twin, but can be a lot more musical than either of them…when he wants to.  On Bikini Bandits, Kill! Kill! Kill!, the buzzkill grinding electro noises and heavy metal riffs are only used sparingly for effect rather than becoming all consuming.

Right off the bat the opener 666 MPH gives a great feel of what’s in store.  Big, slow and brooding, clearly building towards something unknown.  The first time I ever popped the record in, I sat amazed staring at the cover listening to the engine growl and slowed down guitar riff imagining I was in rural Alabama, that the Bikini Bandits were coming to get me and that I couldn’t wait to let them.

Now that track doesn’t even rank in my top 3 on the album.  There’s so much variety going on that if you find one track is too harsh for your palette, just skip to the next, it will be different.  Blood & Fire has an excellent ragga chorus that I sing to myself from time to time.  Can You Dig It is absolutely searing with energy and the first track that screams in your face “this is a break”.  And while I don’t think it’s where he sourced it from, the “can you dig it” battle cry sample must somehow be inspired by the old guy in the California Love video.

My personal favourite (at the moment, it changes all the time) would have to be Dub Murderer.  I’m a sucker for a slow build up because it makes the beat drop so much more incendiary when it happens, so to have no drum hit for three minutes, saving it for all of them at once absolutely kills.  I’d even call the lead up a comforting menace, haunting echoes and drone lightened up with a sprinkling of trumpet and ragga vocals.  Then BAM!  Scattershot insanity, the break breaking itself, turning inside out and feasting on its own rhythm.  I’ve never heard anything like it.

The beat quickly burns itself up and we slide into the cow bell, kick drum and scratching pseudo hip hop of Miss Strap-On USA.  It happens to be another standout track as it’s one of the most accessible, stripped and slowed down on an album that otherwise is none of those three things.

I’ve been using this term all the time lately, but guilty pleasure is probably my best description of this excellent left-field hardcore.  Rough around the edges, but mind-blowing if you can stick through it.

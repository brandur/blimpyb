Fire Braised Chuck Roast

Blimpy On July - 13 - 2010

Who doesn’t love a big ol’ hunk of roast beef?  Well, Hindus I suppose.  But for all us carnivorous mono or non-theists out there, I made up this marinated chuck recipe on the fly, cooked it in a fire pit and it turned out to be the best roast beef I’ve ever had.  Definitely several cuts above grandpa’s shoe leather well-done atrocities.

Serves 4-6

Layin it down

Ingredients

  • 2-3 lb beef chuck roast
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp celery salt
  • 3 tsp mustard powder
  • 12 whole white peppercorns
  • 1 tsp crushed black pepper
  • 3 cloves fresh garlic
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • ¼ can beer
  • 1 oz red Vermouth
  • 1 potato
  • 1 slice onion

Preparation

Finding a good piece of chuck shouldn’t be too hard at your local grocery.  It’s a very marbled cut, just make sure it’s not overly fatty.  If you really feel like showing off the fact you have a job and can afford such luxuries, a prime rib roast would work too.  I opted for the “this is about to go bad” 30% off special pile, it cost me $7, I’m still eating roast beef sandwiches three days later and loving it.

The first step is to get the roast in some sort of dish that can hold about an inch of liquid and be covered in the fridge overnight.  Next, combine all of the dry ingredients over top of the roast.  Roll it around and pat each side until there’s a thin dusting.  Now, cut the fresh garlic into thin, lengthways strips, maybe yielding a total of 15 pieces.  Chop the rosemary into a dozen or so pieces.  Since chuck is a very loose cut held together by string and with several layers/folds within the meat, jam pieces of garlic and rosemary up in there so they’re evenly distributed around the entire roast.

My tire of fire

Now with the seasoned roast in its dish, add the Vermouth and beer (quantity may vary according to pan size and the realization that if in fact you were a Hindu, you’d be that much drunker now).  Once the liquid is added, refrigerate for 24 hours, ideally rotating the roast in the marinade every six hours.  Over time the beer will permeate and break down the tougher chuck meat, as well as enhancing its beef flavour.  I learned recently beer is what gives goulash this flavour, or for the more obvious comparison, Guinness stew.  Sorry if I’ve offended any Irish by mentioning Eastern Europe in the same sentence.

Cooking

This is where it starts to get fun.  Sure, you could toss the thing in a slow cooker or oven for awhile, but it’s been done.  Instead, consider taking it back to basics by cooking it in an open fire pit.  You’ll get superior results from the smoke and flame lickin and be forever revered after your next camping trip or backyard bon fire.

The key to a good cooking fire is simple, get it going strong to start on one side of the pit, let it burn down and maintain while poking coals to the other side where you will cook.  I have heard coals are in the 350 F range, but the meat’s not touching them directly and will get uneven heat regardless.  Before starting the fire, remove the roast from the fridge as going from cold to hot will stress the meat and make it less tender.

Looking good...

Lay out a sheet of tin foil that will encapsulate the whole roast.  Slice the potato and onion (and maybe some more garlic) onto the foil as a bed for the roast, add the meat and bunch up the foil over top.  I chose to leave it open at the top for smokey flavour to get in, but that’s optional.  I also made sure not to rip the foil and poured about 1/3 of the marinade over top of the meat.  Hearing it bubbling 20 minutes in and not having any pour out when I added the rest over time meant success, and the ‘braised’ part of the recipe.

I used tongs to put coals underneath the far side of the tire rim, but I never had more than 10 golf ball sized coals going at once, so ramp that number up if you want a faster cook time.  I turned mine around at 1 hour 45, let it go until the fire had just about died at 3 hours 30, took it out then let it rest for another 10.  Finally cutting into it, I was astounded to find a tender, juicy, even medium well throughout, accented by the aroma of garlic and rosemary.  Needless to say, I proceeded to eat it along with some roasted broccoli and mini portabellos until I didn’t feel good…maybe horseradish would have helped…but still, highly recommended.
